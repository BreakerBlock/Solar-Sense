Rooftop Solar in Karnataka: Cost, Subsidy & Payback

Karnataka's domestic electricity tariff is ₹6.2/kWh and the net-metering buyback rate is ₹3.35/kWh, via BESCOM / HESCOM. Here's what that means for a home going solar. Data updated January 2026.

What a typical 3 kW system looks like in Karnataka

A 3 kW on-grid system suits a Karnataka home using about 477 units a month. At 5.3 peak sun hours it generates roughly 4,526 units a year. The figures below are the solar calculator's own output for Karnataka: it prices the system at its benchmark rate including the net meter, assumes you use 65% of the units yourself at ₹6.2/kWh and export the rest at the ₹3.35/kWh buyback rate. That works out to about ₹23,546 a year and a payback of about 5.6 years after the ₹78,000 PM Surya Ghar subsidy. If your home uses nearly all of the solar units itself, the benefit rises towards ₹28,061 a year and payback falls to about 4.7 years, and a quote below the benchmark shortens it further.

DISCOM & net metering in Karnataka

Your distribution company is BESCOM / HESCOM. To go solar with net metering, you apply for feasibility approval, install through a registered vendor, and get a bi-directional meter. Surplus units you export earn ₹3.35/kWh — lower than the ₹6.2 you pay, which is why self-consuming your generation is always worth more than exporting it.

Is solar worth it in Karnataka?

At ₹6.2/kWh, Karnataka sits in the middle band — solar pays back solidly, especially if you consume most of your generation during the day. Combined with 5.3 peak sun hours a day, the numbers work well. Use the solar subsidy calculator for Karnataka to size a system against your own bill.

Solar in Karnataka: frequently asked questions

How much does a 3 kW rooftop solar system cost in Karnataka?
The solar calculator prices a 3 kW on-grid system at ₹2,09,552 including the net meter; market quotes are typically ₹1.6–2.4 lakh before subsidy (see the solar panel cost guide). After the ₹78,000 PM Surya Ghar subsidy the calculator’s net cost is ₹1,31,552. In Karnataka that system makes about 4,526 units a year, worth about ₹23,546 a year at the ₹6.2/kWh tariff and ₹3.35 buyback, so it pays back in about 5.6 years.
How much solar subsidy can I get in Karnataka?
Households in Karnataka get the central PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana subsidy of ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kW: ₹30,000 for 1 kW, ₹60,000 for 2 kW and a maximum of ₹78,000 for 3 kW or more, worked out pro rata in between (2.5 kW gets ₹69,000). The calculator includes only this central subsidy; ask BESCOM / HESCOM or your state nodal agency whether any state top-up applies when you apply. See the solar subsidy calculator for more sizes.
What size solar system does a home in Karnataka need?
The calculator sizes about 1 kW for every 159 units of monthly use in Karnataka. A typical 2 BHK using 250 units a month needs about 1.6 kW (₹67,494 after subsidy, payback about 5.4 years) and a typical 3 BHK using 450 units needs about 2.9 kW (₹1,26,634, about 5.6 years). Use the average of 12 months of bills rather than a summer month; the 2 BHK and 3 BHK solar guide has more home sizes.
How many units does 1 kW of solar generate in Karnataka?
At 5.3 peak sun hours, 1 kW of panels in Karnataka makes about 4.1 units a day, about 125 a month averaged over the year and about 1,496 a year, after the calculator’s 78% allowance for real-world losses such as heat, dust and wiring. Output is higher in summer and lower in the monsoon, so monthly bills vary; see solar generation per kW by state.
What is the solar buyback rate in Karnataka?
Under net metering with BESCOM / HESCOM, the calculator values exported units at ₹3.35/kWh (published rates as of January 2026), against the ₹6.2/kWh you pay for grid power. Each exported unit is worth about 54% of a unit you use yourself, which is why sizing solar to your own use pays back faster than oversizing for export. For new home systems commissioned from 25 August 2026, the state commission (KERC) has fixed the rate for 25 years at ₹3.89/kWh without capital subsidy, and lower with the PM Surya Ghar subsidy: ₹1.96 up to 2 kW, ₹2.14 above 2 kW up to 3 kW and ₹2.58 above 3 kW. At ₹1.96 a 300-unit home's 1.9 kW system earns ₹1,978 a year on exports instead of ₹3,380, and its payback in the calculator goes from 5.2 to 5.8 years; skipping the subsidy for the ₹3.89 rate would add about ₹1,950 a year but cost ₹57,000 more upfront. Systems connected earlier keep the rate in their agreement, so confirm yours with your ESCOM; net metering explained covers how credits work and compares buyback rates across 22 states.
