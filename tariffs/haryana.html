Haryana's domestic electricity tariff is ₹7.2/kWh and the net-metering buyback rate is ₹2/kWh, via DHBVN / UHBVN. Here's what that means for a home going solar. Data updated January 2026.

What a typical 3 kW system looks like in Haryana

A 3 kW on-grid system suits a Haryana home using about 495 units a month. At 5.5 peak sun hours it generates roughly 4,709 units a year. The figures below are the solar calculator's own output for Haryana: it prices the system at its benchmark rate including the net meter, assumes you use 65% of the units yourself at ₹7.2/kWh and export the rest at the ₹2/kWh buyback rate. That works out to about ₹25,335 a year and a payback of about 5.2 years after the ₹78,000 PM Surya Ghar subsidy. If your home uses nearly all of the solar units itself, the benefit rises towards ₹33,905 a year and payback falls to about 3.9 years, and a quote below the benchmark shortens it further.

DISCOM & net metering in Haryana

Your distribution company is DHBVN / UHBVN. To go solar with net metering, you apply for feasibility approval, install through a registered vendor, and get a bi-directional meter. Surplus units you export earn ₹2/kWh — lower than the ₹7.2 you pay, which is why self-consuming your generation is always worth more than exporting it.

Is solar worth it in Haryana?

With one of India's higher tariffs at ₹7.2/kWh, Haryana offers some of the fastest solar payback in the country. Every unit you generate offsets an expensive grid unit. Combined with 5.5 peak sun hours a day, the numbers are strongly in your favour. Use the solar calculator for Haryana to size a system against your own bill.

Solar in Haryana: frequently asked questions

How much does a 3 kW rooftop solar system cost in Haryana?
The solar calculator prices a 3 kW on-grid system at ₹2,09,552 including the net meter; market quotes are typically ₹1.6–2.4 lakh before subsidy (see the solar panel cost guide). After the ₹78,000 PM Surya Ghar subsidy the calculator’s net cost is ₹1,31,552. In Haryana that system makes about 4,709 units a year, worth about ₹25,335 a year at the ₹7.2/kWh tariff and ₹2 buyback, so it pays back in about 5.2 years.
How much solar subsidy can I get in Haryana?
Households in Haryana get the central PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana subsidy of ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kW: ₹30,000 for 1 kW, ₹60,000 for 2 kW and a maximum of ₹78,000 for 3 kW or more, worked out pro rata in between (2.5 kW gets ₹69,000). The calculator includes only this central subsidy; ask DHBVN / UHBVN or your state nodal agency whether any state top-up applies when you apply. See the solar subsidy calculator for more sizes.
What size solar system does a home in Haryana need?
The calculator sizes about 1 kW for every 165 units of monthly use in Haryana. A typical 2 BHK using 250 units a month needs about 1.6 kW (₹67,494 after subsidy, payback about 5.0 years) and a typical 3 BHK using 450 units needs about 2.8 kW (₹1,21,715, about 5.2 years). Use the average of 12 months of bills rather than a summer month; the 2 BHK and 3 BHK solar guide has more home sizes.
How many units does 1 kW of solar generate in Haryana?
At 5.5 peak sun hours, 1 kW of panels in Haryana makes about 4.3 units a day, about 131 a month averaged over the year and about 1,570 a year, after the calculator’s 78% allowance for real-world losses such as heat, dust and wiring. Output is higher in summer and lower in the monsoon, so monthly bills vary; see solar generation per kW by state.
What is the solar buyback rate in Haryana?
Under net metering with DHBVN / UHBVN, the calculator values exported units at ₹2/kWh (published rates as of January 2026), against the ₹7.2/kWh you pay for grid power. Each exported unit is worth about 28% of a unit you use yourself, which is why sizing solar to your own use pays back faster than oversizing for export. Buyback rates change with tariff orders, so confirm the current rate with your DISCOM; net metering explained covers how credits work.
