What a typical 3 kW system looks like in Haryana

A 3 kW on-grid system suits a Haryana home using about 495 units a month. At 5.5 peak sun hours it generates roughly 4,709 units a year. The figures below are the solar calculator's own output for Haryana: it prices the system at its benchmark rate including the net meter, assumes you use 65% of the units yourself at ₹7.2/kWh and export the rest at the ₹2/kWh buyback rate. That works out to about ₹25,335 a year and a payback of about 5.2 years after the ₹78,000 PM Surya Ghar subsidy. If your home uses nearly all of the solar units itself, the benefit rises towards ₹33,905 a year and payback falls to about 3.9 years, and a quote below the benchmark shortens it further.

DISCOM & net metering in Haryana

Your distribution company is DHBVN / UHBVN. To go solar with net metering, you apply for feasibility approval, install through a registered vendor, and get a bi-directional meter. Surplus units you export earn ₹2/kWh — lower than the ₹7.2 you pay, which is why self-consuming your generation is always worth more than exporting it.

Is solar worth it in Haryana?

With one of India's higher tariffs at ₹7.2/kWh, Haryana offers some of the fastest solar payback in the country. Every unit you generate offsets an expensive grid unit. Combined with 5.5 peak sun hours a day, the numbers are strongly in your favour. Use the solar calculator for Haryana to size a system against your own bill.
