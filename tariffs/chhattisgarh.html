What a typical 3 kW system looks like in Chhattisgarh

A 3 kW on-grid system suits a Chhattisgarh home using about 450 units a month. At 5 peak sun hours it generates roughly 4,271 units a year. The figures below are the solar calculator's own output for Chhattisgarh: it prices the system at its benchmark rate including the net meter, assumes you use 65% of the units yourself at ₹4.8/kWh and export the rest at the ₹2.6/kWh buyback rate. That works out to about ₹17,212 a year and a payback of about 7.6 years after the ₹78,000 PM Surya Ghar subsidy. DISCOM & net metering in Chhattisgarh

Your distribution company is CSPDCL. To go solar with net metering, you apply for feasibility approval, install through a registered vendor, and get a bi-directional meter. Surplus units you export earn ₹2.6/kWh — lower than the ₹4.8 you pay, which is why self-consuming your generation is always worth more than exporting it.

Is solar worth it in Chhattisgarh?

At ₹4.8/kWh, Chhattisgarh's tariff is on the lower side, so payback is slower than high-tariff states. Solar still works, but maximising self-consumption matters more here. Combined with 5 peak sun hours a day, the numbers work well. Use the solar calculator for Chhattisgarh to size a system against your own bill.

Solar in Chhattisgarh: frequently asked questions

How much does a 3 kW rooftop solar system cost in Chhattisgarh?
The solar calculator prices a 3 kW on-grid system at ₹2,09,552 including the net meter; market quotes are typically ₹1.6–2.4 lakh before subsidy (see the solar panel cost guide). After the ₹78,000 PM Surya Ghar subsidy the calculator’s net cost is ₹1,31,552. In Chhattisgarh that system makes about 4,271 units a year, worth about ₹17,212 a year at the ₹4.8/kWh tariff and ₹2.6 buyback, so it pays back in about 7.6 years.
How much solar subsidy can I get in Chhattisgarh?
Households in Chhattisgarh get the central PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana subsidy of ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kW: ₹30,000 for 1 kW, ₹60,000 for 2 kW and a maximum of ₹78,000 for 3 kW or more, worked out pro rata in between (2.5 kW gets ₹69,000). Chhattisgarh also offers its own state subsidy through CSPDCL on top of the central amount. The calculator does not include it, so your net cost can be lower than shown here; check the current amount with CSPDCL. See the solar subsidy calculator for more sizes.
What size solar system does a home in Chhattisgarh need?
The calculator sizes about 1 kW for every 150 units of monthly use in Chhattisgarh. A typical 2 BHK using 250 units a month needs about 1.7 kW (₹71,213 after subsidy, payback about 7.3 years) and a typical 3 BHK using 450 units needs about 3 kW (₹1,31,552, about 7.6 years). Use the average of 12 months of bills rather than a summer month; the 2 BHK and 3 BHK solar guide has more home sizes.
How many units does 1 kW of solar generate in Chhattisgarh?
At 5 peak sun hours, 1 kW of panels in Chhattisgarh makes about 3.9 units a day, about 119 a month averaged over the year and about 1,424 a year, after the calculator’s 78% allowance for real-world losses such as heat, dust and wiring. Output is higher in summer and lower in the monsoon, so monthly bills vary; see solar generation per kW by state.
What is the solar buyback rate in Chhattisgarh?
Under net metering with CSPDCL, the calculator values exported units at ₹2.6/kWh (published rates as of January 2026), against the ₹4.8/kWh you pay for grid power. Each exported unit is worth about 54% of a unit you use yourself, which is why sizing solar to your own use pays back faster than oversizing for export. Buyback rates change with tariff orders, so confirm the current rate with your DISCOM; net metering explained covers how credits work.
