What a typical 3 kW system looks like in West Bengal

A 3 kW on-grid system suits a West Bengal home using about 405 units a month. At 4.5 peak sun hours it generates roughly 3,833 units a year. The figures below are the solar calculator's own output for West Bengal: it prices the system at its benchmark rate including the net meter, assumes you use 65% of the units yourself at ₹7.5/kWh and export the rest at the ₹2.75/kWh buyback rate. That works out to about ₹22,374 a year and a payback of about 5.9 years after the ₹78,000 PM Surya Ghar subsidy. If your home uses nearly all of the solar units itself, the benefit rises towards ₹28,748 a year and payback falls to about 4.6 years, and a quote below the benchmark shortens it further.

DISCOM & net metering in West Bengal

Your distribution company is WBSEDCL / CESC. To go solar with net metering, you apply for feasibility approval, install through a registered vendor, and get a bi-directional meter. Surplus units you export earn ₹2.75/kWh — lower than the ₹7.5 you pay, which is why self-consuming your generation is always worth more than exporting it.

Is solar worth it in West Bengal?

With one of India's higher tariffs at ₹7.5/kWh, West Bengal offers some of the fastest solar payback in the country. Every unit you generate offsets an expensive grid unit. Combined with 4.5 peak sun hours a day, the numbers are workable, though generation is more seasonal. Use the solar panel cost calculator for West Bengal to size a system against your own bill.
