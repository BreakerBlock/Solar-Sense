PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana

India's flagship residential rooftop solar subsidy — up to ₹78,000 credited directly to your bank account. Here are the exact slabs, who qualifies, and how to apply.

Important: ₹78,000 is the absolute ceiling regardless of how large your system is. A 5 kW, 10 kW, or 20 kW system gets the same ₹78,000 as a 3 kW system. The scheme is designed to make the 1–3 kW residential segment affordable — not to subsidise large arrays.

Solar subsidy calculator: how your amount is worked out

The central subsidy depends only on the capacity of your grid-connected rooftop system. It does not change with the price you pay, the panel brand or your state. The slabs are applied pro rata, so part-kilowatt sizes get a proportional amount. This is the same formula our solar subsidy calculator uses:

  • Up to 2 kW: capacity × ₹30,000
  • Between 2 and 3 kW: ₹60,000 + (capacity − 2) × ₹18,000
  • 3 kW or more: ₹78,000, whatever the size

The last column is the useful one. Support per kilowatt is highest up to 2 kW and falls quickly beyond 3 kW, so the subsidy covers a large share of a small system and only a small share of a big one. That doesn't mean you should stop at 3 kW: size the system to your electricity use, because unused capacity only earns your DISCOM's lower buyback rate. The rooftop solar calculator sizes the system from your bill or appliances, applies the subsidy above, and shows your net cost and payback using your state's electricity tariff.

Remember the subsidy is paid after commissioning, so you (or your loan) cover the full cost first; our solar loan and EMI guide shows how to put the subsidy towards the loan when it arrives. State top-ups, where they exist, are added separately and are not included in the calculator's figure.

State solar subsidy top-ups (November 2026)

A few states pay their own subsidy on top of the central PM Surya Ghar amount. These are separate state schemes with their own rules, budgets and deadlines, and our solar subsidy calculator does not add them, so if you live in one of these states your net cost can be lower than the calculator shows. How the amounts stack up, using the central slabs above:

  • Uttar Pradesh: 1 kW gets ₹30,000 + ₹15,000 = ₹45,000; 2 kW gets ₹60,000 + ₹30,000 = ₹90,000; 3 kW or more gets ₹78,000 + ₹30,000 = ₹1,08,000.
  • Delhi: a 3 kW system can get up to ₹78,000 central + ₹78,000 state = ₹1,56,000, and a 2 kW system up to ₹60,000 + ₹52,000 = ₹1,12,000. The state share is limited to the system's cost minus the central subsidy, so the two together never exceed what the system costs. On the free route the subsidies go towards the system rather than to your bank account.
  • Maharashtra (SMART): on a 1 kW system benchmarked at about ₹50,000, the central ₹30,000 plus the state share leaves a BPL household paying about ₹2,500, an SC/ST household about ₹5,000 and other eligible homes about ₹10,000.
  • Rajasthan: the ₹17,000 is added to the central subsidy for your size. Some websites quote ₹95,000 for a 1.1 kW system by adding ₹78,000; the central subsidy for 1.1 kW is ₹33,000, so the total is about ₹50,000.
Other states: some websites list top-ups for Odisha, Bihar, Haryana (low-income households) and a few others, but we could not confirm the amounts from more than one reliable source, so they are not in the table. Ask your DISCOM or state nodal agency when you apply, and check the date of anything you read: Delhi's and Gujarat's schemes changed in September and October 2026.

Who is eligible

  • You must be a residential electricity consumer — not commercial or industrial
  • You must own the roof, or have clear rights to install on it
  • The system must be grid-connected with net metering — off-grid does not qualify
  • Panels and inverter must be from the MNRE ALMM (Approved List of Models and Manufacturers) — your installer handles this
  • You apply through the national portal (pmsuryaghar.gov.in) using a DISCOM-registered vendor
Not eligible: Off-grid systems, commercial/industrial connections, and the housing society common-area scheme (separate RWA rates apply: ₹18,000 per kW, see solar for housing societies). The subsidy is per household — not per system.

How to apply — step by step

1
Register on the portalGo to pmsuryaghar.gov.in. Enter your electricity consumer number and mobile number. Verification is via OTP.
2
Apply for feasibilitySubmit an application to your DISCOM for rooftop solar feasibility approval. They check grid capacity and technical viability.
3
Get the system installedUse a DISCOM-empanelled installer. They must use ALMM-listed panels and inverter. Verify empanelment before signing anything.
4
Apply for net meterYour installer submits the net metering application. The DISCOM installs a bi-directional meter — typically free of charge.
5
Commissioning and inspectionDISCOM inspects and commissions the system. They generate a commissioning certificate.
6
Submit bank details, receive subsidyEnter your bank account on the portal. The central subsidy is credited via Direct Benefit Transfer — typically within 30–45 days of commissioning.
Tip: A good installer files steps 3–5 on your behalf. Ask any prospective vendor: "Who files the subsidy application and net-metering paperwork — you or me?" If they say you, factor in the time cost.

Frequently asked questions

Does the subsidy come before or after I pay?
After. You pay the full system cost upfront (or finance it), and the subsidy lands in your bank account after commissioning and inspection — typically within 30–45 days. Budget for the gross cost and treat the subsidy as a reimbursement.
Why does the calculator show more than ₹78,000?
It shouldn't — we've corrected this. The PM Surya Ghar central subsidy is capped at ₹78,000 for any system 3 kW or larger. Some earlier versions of the calculator incorrectly applied an additional ₹9,000/kW above 3 kW — that figure is wrong. The correct answer is ₹78,000 hard cap.
Can I get the subsidy on a hybrid system?
Yes, on the solar portion only — provided the system is grid-connected, has export capability, and a net meter is installed. The battery is never subsidised. Rules vary by DISCOM; confirm before assuming.
What if my system is larger than 3 kW?
You still get ₹78,000 — the same as a 3 kW system. No additional central subsidy is available above 3 kW. Larger systems fully qualify for net metering and you can sell surplus to the grid, but the extra capacity is self-funded.
Are there state top-up subsidies?
Yes, a few states add their own subsidy on top of the central amount. As of November 2026: Delhi gives up to ₹26,000 per kW (capped at ₹78,000), Uttar Pradesh ₹15,000 per kW (capped at ₹30,000), Gujarat covers the cost above a 10% household share (5% for SC/ST) for systems up to 2.18 kW, Maharashtra's SMART scheme gives ₹10,000–17,500 for 1 kW systems in homes using under 100 units a month, and Rajasthan pays ₹17,000 to households in its 150-unit free electricity scheme. The calculator includes only the central subsidy. See state solar subsidy top-ups for the conditions.
Is rooftop solar free in Delhi?
For many homes, yes. Under the September 2026 amendment to the Delhi Solar Energy Policy, residential consumers who averaged up to 400 units a month can get a system of up to 3 kW at no cost through the government's utility-led route, with the central and state subsidies and any extra state support covering the price. Homes above 400 units, or those installing through their own vendor, can still get up to ₹78,000 central plus up to ₹78,000 state subsidy for 3 kW. Check your eligibility on the Delhi solar portal or with BSES / Tata Power Delhi.
