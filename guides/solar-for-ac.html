How Much Solar Do You Need to Run an AC in India?

In most Indian homes the air conditioner is the biggest single load on the bill, and the main reason people look at rooftop solar. Here is how many units a 1.5 ton AC really uses, how many kW of panels cover it, what that costs after subsidy, what changes if you want to run it at night, and how the other appliances in a typical home add up.

Quick answer: a modern 1.5 ton 5-star inverter AC running 8 hours a day uses about 1 unit (kWh) an hour, or roughly 240 units a month. Covering that takes about 1.5–1.6 kW of rooftop solar (1.5 kW in Delhi, 1.6 kW at the 5-hour Indian average). An older non-inverter 1.5 ton AC uses about 1.5–1.8 units an hour and needs roughly 2.2–2.7 kW in Delhi for the same hours.

How many units does a 1.5 ton AC use?

An AC's nameplate wattage is its draw at full power. An inverter AC runs at full power only until the room cools, then slows its compressor down, so its average draw over an evening is well below the nameplate figure. A fixed-speed (non-inverter) AC switches fully on and off and averages closer to its rated draw. Typical real-world figures for a 1.5 ton split AC in Indian conditions, once the room has cooled:

1.5 ton AC typeUnits per hour (average)Units per month at 8 h/day
5-star inverter~0.8–1.1~190–265
3-star inverter~1.0–1.3~240–310
Non-inverter or older window AC~1.5–1.8~360–430

Use rises on very hot days, with a low thermostat setting, in a room with a lot of sun or poor insulation, and during the first few minutes after switching on. The yearly figure printed on the BEE star label is measured under standard test conditions over an assumed 1,600 hours of use a year, so it usually comes out lower per hour than what people see in a hot Indian summer. If you can, check your own AC: compare your bill's units in a month with heavy AC use against a month without it.

Payback is barely affected by AC type because the system grows with the load; what changes is the size of the investment and how much roof it needs (about 83 sq ft for 1.5 kW, 132 sq ft for 2.4 kW).

In a state with less sun the same AC needs more panels. At the 5-hour average a 5-star inverter AC at 8 hours a day needs 1.6 kW; in Kerala or West Bengal (4.5 h) it needs 1.8 kW. The solar generation per kW guide has every state's sun hours.

One honest caveat: dividing by peak sun hours, as the calculator does, covers about 78–80% of the AC's yearly units once real-world losses are counted (heat, dust, wiring and inverter losses take about 22% off). To cover all of it, divide the size by 0.78: a 5-star AC at 8 hours a day in Delhi then needs about 1.9 kW instead of 1.5 kW.

How many hours a day you run it matters most

A 5-star inverter AC at about 1 unit an hour, at the 5-hour Indian average sun (₹7/kWh tariff and ₹2.50 buyback, the calculator's defaults when no state is chosen):

Size for the year, not for May. Most homes run the AC hard for a few hot months and little or not at all the rest of the year, while solar panels produce all year. Under net metering the units you export in mild months are credited against your bill, subject to your DISCOM's settlement rules, so size for your average monthly units across the whole year. Eight hours a day for six months, for example, averages out to about 120 units a month over the year: the 0.8 kW row, not the 1.6 kW one. The simplest way to get this right is to enter your average monthly units from a year of bills in the calculator's bill mode. See net metering explained.

Can solar run an AC at night?

Most AC use is in the evening and at night, when panels produce nothing. How that works depends on the type of system:

  • On-grid (most homes). The panels feed your home and the grid during the day, and the AC runs on grid power at night. Net metering offsets the two in units on your bill, so the panels still pay for the AC's electricity. The calculator is cautious here: it assumes 65% of your solar is used directly and values the rest at the lower buyback rate, so true savings under full net metering can be higher. See the on-grid solar guide.
  • Off-grid. Every unit the AC uses at night has to come from a battery charged during the day. For the same 5-star AC at 8 hours a night in Delhi, the calculator's off-grid mode sizes 2.0 kW of panels plus a 14.1 kWh lithium (LFP) battery for 1.5 days of backup: about ₹5.06 lakh with no subsidy, against ₹63,776 after subsidy for the on-grid version. The battery alone is ₹3.95 lakh. See the off-grid solar guide.
  • Hybrid. A grid-tied system with a battery can keep essentials running during power cuts. Backing up an AC through long outages needs a large battery and an inverter rated for the AC's starting current, so most hybrid owners keep the AC on the grid and back up fans, lights, the fridge and Wi-Fi. See the hybrid solar guide.

Can a 1 kW system "run" a 1.5 ton AC on its own? Not in real time: 1 kW of panels delivers under 1,000 W even at noon, which is less than the AC's draw at full speed, so on an on-grid system the grid tops up the difference moment to moment. Over a day, though, 1 kW makes about 3.9 units at average Indian sun, enough to cover about 4 hours of a 5-star inverter AC.

Cutting the AC's units before you size solar

Every unit the AC doesn't use is a unit you don't need panels for. A few things make a real difference:

  • Thermostat. Set it a few degrees higher, around 24–26°C, and use a ceiling fan with it. The compressor works much less hard.
  • Star rating. If you are replacing an old AC anyway, a 5-star inverter model uses roughly a third less than a non-inverter one, which at 8 hours a day means about 0.9 kW less solar in Delhi (1.5 kW instead of 2.4 kW).
  • Cleaning and servicing. Clogged filters and dirty coils make the AC run longer for the same cooling.
  • Shade and insulation. Curtains on sun-facing windows and closed doors cut the heat the AC has to remove. Panels on the roof also shade it, which can make the top-floor rooms slightly cooler.
  • Timing. Pre-cooling in the afternoon, when the panels are producing, uses solar directly instead of through net metering, which is worth more where exported units are paid at a low buyback rate rather than offset at your full tariff.

Frequently asked questions

How many kW of solar do I need for a 1.5 ton AC?
For 8 hours a day, about 1.5–1.6 kW for a 5-star inverter AC (about 1 unit an hour): 1.5 kW in Delhi and 1.6 kW at the 5-hour Indian average sun. A 3-star inverter AC needs about 1.8 kW and an older non-inverter AC about 2.2–2.7 kW in Delhi. Fewer hours or only a few months of AC use a year need less.
How many units does a 1.5 ton AC use per month?
At 8 hours a day, roughly 190–265 units a month for a 5-star inverter AC, 240–310 for a 3-star inverter AC and 360–430 for a non-inverter or older window AC. Very hot weather, a low thermostat setting and a sunny room push it higher.
Can a 1 kW solar system run a 1.5 ton AC?
Not on its own in real time: 1 kW of panels delivers less than the AC's full-speed draw, so on an on-grid system the grid supplies the difference. Over a day, 1 kW makes about 3.9 units at average Indian sun, which covers about 4 hours of a 5-star inverter AC.
Can I run my AC on solar at night?
On an on-grid system, yes in effect: the AC runs on grid power at night and net metering offsets it against the units your panels exported during the day. Running it directly from solar at night needs a battery. For a 5-star AC at 8 hours a night in Delhi, the calculator's off-grid mode sizes 2.0 kW of panels and a 14.1 kWh lithium battery, about ₹5.06 lakh in total with no subsidy, compared with ₹63,776 after subsidy for on-grid.
