Solar Panel Types in India: Mono PERC vs TOPCon vs Bifacial

Higher efficiency means less roof area for the same output — which matters most when space is tight. Here's how the four common panel types stack up for Indian rooftops, what they cost per watt in 2026, and what each one does to a real system's price and payback.

Quick answer: for most Indian homes the choice in 2026 is between Mono PERC and TOPCon. TOPCon needs about 11% less roof, handles heat a little better and degrades more slowly, and its price premium has shrunk to roughly ₹1–2 per watt for the DCR panels the PM Surya Ghar subsidy requires. In the calculator, a 3 kW Delhi system costs ₹1,31,552 after subsidy with Mono PERC and ₹1,43,018 with TOPCon; both make the same 4,709 units a year. Polycrystalline is rarely quoted for homes now, and bifacial only pays on raised structures over a light-coloured roof or ground.

Solar panel price per watt in 2026 (DCR and non-DCR)

Panel prices in India now depend as much on whether the cells are made in India as on the technology. Panels with domestic cells are called DCR (domestic content requirement) panels, and only DCR panels qualify for the PM Surya Ghar subsidy. At bulk (factory-gate) level the gap between TOPCon and PERC was only about ₹0.40 per watt for non-DCR panels in April 2026, which is why many installers now quote TOPCon at little or no extra cost. Panels are only part of the bill: for whole-system prices per kW see the solar panel cost guide.

DCR and the 2026 cell rules: a subsidised home system must use DCR panels installed by a vendor registered on the national portal. Since 1 June 2026 the government's approved list for solar cells (ALMM List-II) also applies to net-metered systems; MNRE's August 2026 clarification exempts net-metered projects commissioned on or before 31 December 2026, and does not relax DCR rules for subsidy schemes. If you are not claiming the subsidy and plan to commission in 2027, ask your installer whether the panels quoted comply.

Heat, degradation and the 25-year view

Panels lose output as they get hotter, and on an Indian rooftop in May the cells can run 30–40°C above the 25°C at which they are rated. The loss per degree is the panel's temperature coefficient, printed on the datasheet: about −0.34%/°C for Mono PERC and −0.30%/°C for TOPCon in manufacturers' 2026 specifications. At a cell temperature of 60°C that is a loss of about 11.9% for PERC against 10.5% for TOPCon.

TOPCon also degrades more slowly: one manufacturer's 2026 warranty terms allow about 1% in the first year and 0.40% a year after that for TOPCon, against about 2% and 0.45% a year for Mono PERC. One installer's 2026 modelling for Jaipur and Mumbai put TOPCon's extra generation at around 2% in the first year and 2.5–3% over 25 years.

What that is worth: for the Delhi 3 kW system above, 2.5% more generation is about 118 units a year, or roughly ₹680 a year at the calculator's blended value of ₹5.78 per unit — about ₹17,000 over 25 years without any tariff increase. At 2026 retail prices the DCR TOPCon premium for 3 kW is roughly ₹3,000–6,000, so TOPCon usually earns back its premium. The calculator does not model this extra yield, so treat it as upside.

Check the datasheet of the exact panel you are quoted rather than relying on the type: temperature coefficients, first-year degradation and warranty terms differ between manufacturers and product lines.

Polycrystalline

The blue-tinted budget option. Lowest cost per watt, but the lowest efficiency means it needs the most roof space and tolerates heat least well. Fine when space is plentiful and budget is the priority, but in 2026 most price lists for home rooftops cover only Mono PERC and TOPCon, so you may not be offered it — and a DCR polycrystalline panel for the subsidy is hard to find.

Mono PERC

Black cells, the current industry standard and the default most installers quote. The best balance of cost and efficiency, widely available with good supply and service. For most homes, this is the sensible pick.

TOPCon N-Type

Newer N-type cells with better low-light and high-temperature performance and slower degradation — meaning more output in year 25. The 30-year warranty and low degradation make it attractive if you plan to stay long-term, at roughly 20% more than mono.

Bifacial HJT

Generates from both faces, so it needs elevated mounting or a reflective surface below to capture the bonus. On rooftops the rear-side gain is modest; on ground mounts it can add up to 12% yield. Highest cost, best for space-constrained sites or ground installations where the extra output is worth the premium. As a rough guide from 2026 supplier data, the rear-side gain is only about 2–3% on panels mounted flat and low over a dark roof, and about 5–7% on ground mounts a metre or more above a light surface; a raised structure over a white or light-coloured terrace sits in between. The calculator's bifacial option prices the panels but does not add rear-side yield.

Which should you choose?

  • Plenty of space, tight budget: Polycrystalline or Mono PERC
  • Most homes: Mono PERC — the default for good reason
  • Limited roof, want maximum output: TOPCon or Bifacial
  • Ground mount: Bifacial earns its premium here

Frequently asked questions

Which is better for a home in India, TOPCon or Mono PERC?
For most homes in 2026, TOPCon. It needs about 11% less roof space for the same kW, loses less output in heat (about −0.30%/°C against −0.34%/°C) and degrades more slowly, while DCR TOPCon panels cost only about ₹1–2 per watt more than DCR Mono PERC (₹25–30/W against ₹24–28/W in an October 2026 price guide). Mono PERC is still a sound choice if a good installer quotes it noticeably cheaper.
What is the price of a solar panel per watt in India in 2026?
An October 2026 retail price guide put Mono PERC at ₹18–23/W non-DCR and ₹24–28/W DCR, and TOPCon at ₹21–26/W non-DCR and ₹25–30/W DCR, including 5% GST. Bifacial panels cost about ₹1–2/W more than single-sided panels of the same cell type. Only DCR panels qualify for the PM Surya Ghar subsidy.
Is a bifacial solar panel worth it on a rooftop?
Usually only on a raised structure over a white or light-coloured roof. Mounted flat and low over a dark roof, the rear side adds only about 2–3%; on ground mounts a metre or more above a light surface it is about 5–7%. If your panels will sit close to the roof, a single-sided TOPCon panel is normally better value.
Which solar panels qualify for the PM Surya Ghar subsidy?
DCR panels (with Indian-made cells) from the government's approved list, installed by a vendor registered on the national portal. Mono PERC and TOPCon panels are both available in DCR versions. The panel type does not change the subsidy amount, which depends only on system size: ₹30,000 per kW up to 2 kW, ₹18,000 for the third kW, and ₹78,000 at most.
How much roof space does a 3 kW system need with each panel type?
The panels alone cover about 202 sq ft with polycrystalline, 166 sq ft with Mono PERC, 147 sq ft with TOPCon and 140 sq ft with bifacial HJT, using the calculator's efficiencies. Including walkways and shade-free spacing, plan for roughly 240–300 sq ft of usable roof for 3 kW.
