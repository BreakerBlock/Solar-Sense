Solar Panel Cost in India (2026): Price per kW and After Subsidy

How much does rooftop solar cost in India in 2026? For a home, a complete on-grid system is typically quoted at ₹55,000–85,000 per kW installed, and the PM Surya Ghar subsidy takes up to ₹78,000 off. Here are prices for 1 kW to 10 kW, what goes into the number, and how to read a quote.

In one line: a 3 kW home system is typically quoted at ₹1.65–2.55 lakh before subsidy in 2026. After the ₹78,000 central subsidy, most homes pay about ₹1–1.5 lakh.

Solar cost per kW in 2026

Published 2026 price guides put a complete residential on-grid system at about ₹55–85 per watt installed, or ₹55,000–85,000 per kW. That figure includes panels, inverter, mounting structure, wiring and protection, and installation. The spread comes from panel type and origin, inverter quality, how high and how strong the structure is, your city, and how much paperwork the installer handles.

Our solar panel cost calculator uses a benchmark of ₹65,000 per kW for on-grid systems, adjusted for panel technology, plus ₹8,000 for the net meter. With Mono PERC panels that comes to about ₹67,200 per kW before the meter, near the middle of the market range. Its estimates are meant to be a fair reference point, not the lowest quote you will find.

Two things stand out. First, the subsidy is worth the most per kW on small systems. It covers ₹30,000 a kW up to 2 kW, ₹18,000 for the third kW, and stops growing at 3 kW, so the net price per kW rises from about ₹41,000 at 2 kW to about ₹60,000 at 10 kW. Second, larger systems often get a lower per-kW quote before subsidy, because installation and paperwork costs are spread over more panels. The calculator does not assume that discount for homes, which is one reason its estimates for 6–10 kW can sit above competitive quotes.

What you save matters as much as what you pay. For a Delhi home (₹7/kWh) with each system sized to its own consumption, the calculator's payback ranges from about 4.5 to 6.7 years across 1–10 kW, shortest at 2–3 kW where the subsidy covers the largest share. Panels are the largest single item but well under half the bill. That is why a quote that looks cheap because of a low panel price can still be expensive overall, and why the structure, cabling and inverter deserve as much scrutiny as the panel brand.

Solar panel price per watt: DCR vs non-DCR

Panel prices are quoted per watt. Retail prices seen in late 2026 are roughly:

Panel typeTypical retail priceWhere it is used
Non-DCR (imported cells)₹18–26 per wattHomes not claiming the subsidy, commercial roofs
DCR (Indian-made cells)₹24–34 per wattRequired for the PM Surya Ghar subsidy

DCR (domestic content requirement) panels use cells made in India. PM Surya Ghar requires them, so every subsidised home system uses DCR panels and carries their higher price. The subsidy more than covers the difference on a home system: on 3 kW, a DCR premium of around ₹6–10 per watt adds roughly ₹18,000–30,000, against ₹78,000 of subsidy. Homes that skip the subsidy, and businesses, can use non-DCR panels.

Panel technology also changes the price. In the calculator, TOPCon panels add 22% to the panel line, which raises a 3 kW system from ₹2,09,552 to ₹2,21,018 (about 5.5% more), while needing about 147 sq ft of panels instead of 166 sq ft. Hybrid and off-grid: what batteries add

Batteries are the biggest extra cost in solar. 2026 prices for home storage run about ₹20,000–35,000 per kWh for lithium (LFP) and ₹8,000–15,000 per kWh for lead-acid. Lithium costs more upfront but lasts much longer and can be discharged more deeply. The calculator prices LFP at ₹28,000 and tubular lead-acid at ₹12,000 per kWh, both inside those ranges.

Adding battery backup to that 3 kW system for a home using about 495 units a month in Delhi sizes a 6.8 kWh LFP bank, adding ₹1,90,400 and taking the total before subsidy to about ₹4.0 lakh. The battery itself is not subsidised, and payback stretches from 4.8 to about 11.8 years. That is why most homes with a reliable grid choose plain on-grid solar, and add a small battery only if power cuts are frequent. How to compare solar quotes

  • Compare price per kW, not the total. Divide each quote by its system size, using the panels' rated capacity in kWp.
  • Check the panels are DCR if you want the subsidy, and that the installer is registered on the PM Surya Ghar national portal.
  • Ask for an itemised quote: panel make, wattage and count; inverter make, capacity and warranty; structure material and height; cable sizes; earthing and lightning protection.
  • Confirm what is included: net meter, DISCOM application and inspection fees, transport, civil work and any annual maintenance contract.
  • Read the warranties: panels typically carry 25–30 years on output; inverters usually far less, so the inverter warranty and service network matter.
  • Get at least three quotes, and check them against the calculator's estimate for your bill and state.

Frequently asked questions

What is the price of a 1 kW solar system in India in 2026?
A complete 1 kW on-grid system is typically quoted at ₹55,000–85,000 in 2026. The calculator estimates ₹75,184 with Mono PERC panels, including the ₹8,000 net meter. After the ₹30,000 PM Surya Ghar subsidy, that is about ₹45,000.
How much does a 5 kW solar system cost after subsidy?
Quotes for 5 kW typically run ₹2.75–4.25 lakh before subsidy. The calculator estimates ₹3,43,920 with Mono PERC panels. The subsidy stops at ₹78,000 from 3 kW upwards, so the net cost is about ₹2.66 lakh.
Why are DCR solar panels more expensive?
DCR panels use solar cells made in India, which currently cost more than imported cells. Retail prices in late 2026 are about ₹24–34 per watt for DCR panels against ₹18–26 for non-DCR. The PM Surya Ghar subsidy requires DCR panels, and on a home system it is worth far more than the price difference.
Why does my installer's quote differ from the calculator?
The calculator uses benchmark rates of ₹65,000 per kW on-grid, adjusted for panel type, so it lands near the middle of the market. Real quotes vary with city, roof type, structure height, panel and inverter brands, and the installer's margin. A quote far below the range deserves a close look at what has been left out.
