Hybrid Solar in India: Battery Size, Backup Hours & Cost

The middle path: grid-tied like on-grid, but with a small battery to keep essentials running during outages. You get export income and subsidy, plus lights and a fridge when the power cuts.

Best for: Urban homes with occasional but disruptive outages, home offices, or anyone with medical equipment that can't lose power.

What "hybrid" means

A hybrid system is grid-tied with a battery. It exports surplus to the grid like an on-grid system, but keeps a small battery — usually 2–5 kWh — to run essential loads during outages: lights, fans, fridge, Wi-Fi router.

The battery is deliberately undersized. You're not trying to run the air conditioner through a six-hour cut; you're keeping the fridge cold and the lights on. That keeps the cost far below a true off-grid system while still solving the on-grid blackout problem.

A hybrid inverter does three jobs in one box: it converts panel power for the house and the grid, charges the battery, and switches your backed-up circuits to the battery when the grid fails (a plain on-grid inverter shuts down in an outage for line-worker safety). Panels and net metering work exactly as on an on-grid system, so the panel size is still set by your monthly units — see how many solar panels you need.

How big a battery do you need?

Size the battery for the loads you want to keep running and for how long, not for your whole bill:

Battery size (kWh) = backed-up load (kW) × backup hours ÷ usable depth of discharge ÷ ~0.9 inverter efficiency
Usable depth of discharge is about 85% for lithium iron phosphate (LFP) and 50% for lead-acid tubular batteries — the same figures the calculator uses.

The three load sets below use the calculator's own appliance wattages (LED bulb 10 W, ceiling fan 75 W, refrigerator 150 W, TV 100 W, laptop 60 W) plus about 10 W for a router. A fridge's compressor cycles on and off, so its real average draw is often lower than 150 W; treat these figures as a safe upper estimate.

What it costs

The premium over on-grid goes almost entirely to the battery and a hybrid-capable inverter, so it depends mainly on battery size. Price surveys from September–October 2026 put home batteries at roughly:

  • Lithium (LFP): about ₹16,000–35,000 per kWh installed, with larger packs at the lower end; rated for about 2,000–6,000 cycles, or roughly 8–15 years of service
  • Lead-acid tubular: about ₹8,000–16,000 per kWh, but only half of it is usable and it typically lasts 3–5 years
  • Hybrid inverter: one October 2026 survey put a 3 kW hybrid inverter at about ₹44,000–1.02 lakh before batteries, against ₹20,000–32,000 for a comparable on-grid inverter
  • Complete hybrid system with battery: about ₹2.1–3.5 lakh for 3 kW and ₹3.3–5.5 lakh for 5 kW before subsidy in the same survey, compared with roughly ₹1.6–2.4 lakh for a 3 kW on-grid system (see the solar panel cost guide)

Over 25 years of panel life you should expect to replace an LFP battery once and a lead-acid battery several times, so compare quotes on lifetime cost, not just the upfront price.

In other words, a hybrid system is backup you pay for, not a better investment. It makes sense when outages cost you something real — lost work, spoiled food, a medical device — or when you already pay for an inverter-battery that the solar battery would replace.

Does it qualify for subsidy?

In September 2024 MNRE clarified that residential rooftop solar systems with battery storage are also eligible for central financial assistance under PM Surya Ghar, and that hybrid inverters can be installed as allowed by your state electricity regulator's rules. The subsidy is still worked out per kW of solar capacity — ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW, ₹18,000 for the third, capped at ₹78,000 — so adding a battery does not increase it. You still need a grid connection, installation through a vendor registered on the national portal, and your DISCOM's approval (normally with a net meter), and rules differ by state, so confirm with your DISCOM and vendor before you sign. The solar subsidy calculator counts the subsidy on the solar capacity only, never the battery, when you add battery backup to an on-grid system. Fully off-grid systems that are not connected to the grid get no subsidy.

Where hybrid fits

  • You want backup for essentials, not full independence
  • Your outages are occasional but disruptive — a few hours, a few times a week
  • You run a home office, or have medical equipment that can't lose power
  • You still want export income and the subsidy

If your outages are rare and short, plain on-grid is the better buy; if you have no reliable grid at all, look at off-grid sizing instead.

Hybrid solar FAQ

How many hours of backup does a hybrid solar battery give?
It depends on the battery and what you run. A 5.12 kWh LFP battery gives about 3.9 kWh of usable energy, which runs 10 LED lights, two fans, a router and a refrigerator (about 410 W) for roughly 9–10 hours, or the same loads plus two more fans, a TV and a laptop (about 720 W) for about 5 hours. A 2.56 kWh LFP battery runs the 410 W set for about 4–5 hours.
What size battery do I need for a 3 kW hybrid solar system?
The panel size does not decide the battery; your backup needs do. For lights, fans, fridge and router for about 4 hours, a 2–3 kWh LFP battery is enough. The calculator, which sizes the battery to store about 35% of daily use, adds 6.2 kWh to the 2.8 kW system for a Delhi home using 450 units a month.
How much does a hybrid solar system cost in India?
An October 2026 price survey put a complete hybrid system with battery at about ₹2.1–3.5 lakh for 3 kW and ₹3.3–5.5 lakh for 5 kW before subsidy. In the calculator, a Delhi home using 300 units a month pays ₹78,650 after subsidy for 1.9 kW on-grid and ₹1,93,450 with a 4.1 kWh LFP battery added.
Is a hybrid solar system eligible for the PM Surya Ghar subsidy?
Yes, if it is grid-connected and installed under the scheme. MNRE clarified in September 2024 that rooftop systems with battery storage are eligible and that hybrid inverters can be used as allowed by state regulations. The subsidy is calculated on the solar capacity (₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW, ₹18,000 for the third, up to ₹78,000), so the battery does not add to it. Confirm the details with your DISCOM.
Is hybrid solar worth it compared with on-grid?
Only if you value backup. The battery adds cost but no bill savings, so in the calculator a Delhi home using 300 units a month sees payback rise from 4.6 years on-grid to 11.2 years with a battery. If outages cost you work, food or comfort, or the solar battery replaces an inverter-battery you would buy anyway, it can be worth it; otherwise on-grid pays back faster.
