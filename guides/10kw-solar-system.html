10 kW Solar System for Home in India: Price, Units, Subsidy and Payback

A 10 kW rooftop system is the size large homes look at when bills run into four figures of units: several ACs, an EV, a big family under one roof. Here is what a 10 kW on-grid system costs in 2026, how many units it makes, what the PM Surya Ghar subsidy covers, how long it takes to pay back in each state, and what your connection needs before you can install one.

Quick answer: a 10 kW system makes about 43 units a day, or ~1,300 units a month in Delhi, and suits homes using roughly 1,350–1,950 units a month depending on how sunny the state is. 2026 quotes mostly run ₹5–7 lakh before subsidy. The home subsidy is capped at ₹78,000, the same as for 3 kW. In Delhi the calculator puts the payback at 6.7 years, or about 5.2 years on a competitive ₹5.5 lakh quote. Most DISCOMs will want a sanctioned load of about 10 kW and, usually, a three-phase connection.

Homes that get there usually have several air conditioners running through summer, an electric car charged at home, electric water heating or cooking, or two or three households on one meter. Check a full year of bills before settling on 10 kW. A home that uses 2,000 units in May but 900 in January can average well under 1,650 over the year, and under net metering the system only has to match the yearly total, not the peak month. 10 kW solar price in 2026

Quotes published in 2026 for a complete 10 kW on-grid home system mostly fall between ₹5 lakh and ₹7 lakh before subsidy, about ₹50,000–70,000 per kW. That is lower per kW than small systems, because the inverter, wiring, paperwork and labour are spread over more panels. DCR panels (needed for the subsidy), an elevated structure and premium modules push quotes to the upper end.

The calculator does not apply that size discount to homes: it uses ₹65,000 per kW for every on-grid system plus ₹8,000 for the net meter, which gives ₹6,79,840 for 10 kW. Treat that as a conservative ceiling. Our solar panel cost guide has the full 1–10 kW price list and what goes into a quote.

The PM Surya Ghar subsidy for a home is ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kW, and it stops at ₹78,000 at 3 kW. A 10 kW system gets the same ₹78,000, about 11–16% of a typical quote. Some states pay a top-up of their own on top of the central amount; check your state's solar portal, as the calculator does not include state top-ups.

Is 10 kW better than 5 kW?

Only if you use the units (the 5 kW solar system guide covers the smaller size in detail). Going from 5 kW to 10 kW in Delhi adds ₹3,35,920 to the calculator's net cost (₹6,01,840 − ₹2,65,920), because the extra 5 kW gets no subsidy at all.

  • If your home really uses ~1,650 units a month, the yearly benefit rises from ₹45,108 (5 kW sized for 825 units) to ₹90,430, an extra ₹45,322 a year. The extra 5 kW pays for itself in about 7.4 years.
  • If your home uses ~825 units a month and you install 10 kW anyway, the extra ~7,850 units a year are mostly surplus. Surplus beyond your own use is usually settled at the buyback rate; at Delhi's ₹3.50 that is about ₹27,500 a year, so the extra 5 kW takes roughly 12 years to pay back. Rules differ by state; see net metering explained.

The middle sizes behave the same way. In Delhi the calculator gives 6 kW (990 units a month) a payback of 6.1 years, 7 kW (1,155 units) 6.3 years and 8 kW (1,320 units) 6.5 years, each sized to its own use.

Connection, sanctioned load and approvals

  • Sanctioned load. National net-metering rules allow a rooftop system up to your sanctioned load, so a home on a 5 kW connection usually has to apply for a load enhancement to about 10 kW first, with a one-time charge. Some states have relaxed this for homes; check your DISCOM's current rules.
  • Three-phase supply. Most installers fit a three-phase inverter at 10 kW, and DISCOMs commonly require a three-phase connection for systems of this size. The exact limit for single-phase supply is set by each state's supply code, so ask your DISCOM before you sign.
  • Feasibility. Since the 2024 amendment to the consumer rules, rooftop systems up to 10 kW need no technical feasibility study by the DISCOM, which keeps approval simple. Go even slightly above 10 kW and the study applies (it must be completed within 15 days).
  • Subsidy conditions. To claim the ₹78,000 you apply on the national PM Surya Ghar portal, use a registered vendor and DCR (made-in-India cell) panels. The subsidy goes to one residential connection; it does not scale up for a bigger system.

Roof space and panels

Ten kW of Mono PERC panels covers about 552 sq ft. Allow 800–1,000 sq ft of shadow-free roof once walkways and row spacing are added, more if water tanks, stair rooms or parapets cast shade. On a smaller roof, higher-efficiency TOPCon panels or an elevated structure over the terrace can help. The panel count guide explains how to work out the number of panels and space for any size, and solar panel types compared covers efficiency.

Paying for it with a loan

Scheme loans for systems above 3 kW are linked to home-loan rates, roughly 8–10%, with a 20% margin. As an illustration, a ₹5 lakh loan over 10 years at 9% costs about ₹6,334 a month, against the calculator's average Delhi saving of about ₹7,536 a month for a 10 kW system sized to 1,650 units. Use the solar loan and EMI guide to try your own amount, rate and tenure.

Before you sign for 10 kW

  1. Add up 12 months of units and divide by your state's sunshine (the table above) to confirm you need 10 kW, not 6–8 kW.
  2. Check your sanctioned load and phase on the bill, and ask the DISCOM what a load enhancement involves.
  3. Get at least three quotes for the same kW, panel type (DCR or not), inverter type and structure height, and compare the price per kW.
  4. Ask for the expected units per year in writing and compare it with the figures here.
  5. Confirm who handles the net meter, the subsidy application and after-sales service, and for how long.

Frequently asked questions

What is the price of a 10 kW solar system in India?
Quotes published in 2026 for a complete 10 kW on-grid home system mostly run ₹5–7 lakh before subsidy, about ₹50,000–70,000 per kW. The calculator's benchmark estimate is ₹6,79,840 with Mono PERC panels. After the ₹78,000 PM Surya Ghar subsidy, the net cost is about ₹4.2–6.2 lakh.
How many units does a 10 kW solar system generate per day?
About 35–51 units a day averaged over the year, depending on the state: around 43 units a day (about 1,300 a month, 15,659 a year) in Delhi or Maharashtra, 35 in Kerala and 51 in Rajasthan. Summer months produce more and monsoon or winter-haze months less.
How much subsidy is available on a 10 kW solar system?
For a home, the central PM Surya Ghar subsidy is capped at ₹78,000, the same amount as for a 3 kW system, because it covers only the first 3 kW. Some states add a top-up of their own. Housing societies get a different rate for common facilities.
Does a 10 kW solar system need a three-phase connection?
Usually yes. Installers fit a three-phase inverter at this size and DISCOMs commonly require three-phase supply for it, though the exact rule is set by your state's supply code. You will also normally need a sanctioned load of about 10 kW, so check your bill and ask your DISCOM before you sign.
