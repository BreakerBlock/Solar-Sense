1 kW and 2 kW Solar System for Home: Price, Units, Subsidy and Payback

Small rooftop systems are where most Indian homes start: a 1 kW or 2 kW on-grid system for a household using roughly 130–400 units a month. They are also where the PM Surya Ghar subsidy is most generous, at ₹30,000 for every kW. Here is what 1 kW and 2 kW systems cost in 2026, how many units they make in each state, how long they take to pay back, and when 1.5, 2.5 or 3 kW is the better buy.

Quick answer: a 1 kW system makes about 4.3 units a day (~130 a month) in Delhi and a 2 kW system about 8.6 units a day (~260 a month). They suit homes using roughly 135–195 and 270–390 units a month respectively, depending on how sunny the state is. The subsidy is ₹30,000 for 1 kW and ₹60,000 for 2 kW. The calculator prices them at ₹75,184 and ₹1,42,368 before subsidy, a net ₹45,184 and ₹82,368, and in Delhi puts the payback at 4.5 years for 2 kW, the shortest of any home size, and 5.0 years for 1 kW.

That lines up with the usual rule of thumb for the scheme, reported in 2026 as roughly 1 kW for homes using under 150 units a month and 2–3 kW for 150–300 units. A home using 150–300 units a month with a fridge, fans, lights, a TV and a washing machine but no air conditioner typically sits in this range. Add a 1.5 ton AC through summer and you will usually need 3 kW or more; solar for AC and appliances shows how much each appliance adds.

Use a full year of bills, not one month. Under net metering the system only has to match your yearly total, and a system sized this way produces about 79% of a year's use after real-world losses (see how many units 1 kW generates). If you want closer to 100%, size up by a quarter, which for most homes means moving from 2 kW to 2.5 kW.

1 kW and 2 kW solar price in 2026

Published 2026 price guides vary widely for small systems. Basic 1 kW on-grid packages are quoted from about ₹45,000–65,000, and 2 kW from about ₹85,000–1.25 lakh, before subsidy. Full-service packages at the top of the market are quoted well above that, up to about ₹1.2–1.3 lakh for 1 kW and ₹1.8–1.9 lakh for 2 kW. Panel type (subsidised systems must use DCR, made-in-India-cell panels), inverter brand, structure height and whether the net meter and DISCOM fees are included explain most of the gap.

The calculator uses one benchmark for every home: ₹65,000 per kW plus ₹8,000 for the net meter. That gives ₹75,184 for 1 kW and ₹1,42,368 for 2 kW with Mono PERC panels, in the middle of the published range. The fixed meter cost is why 1 kW costs more per kW than larger systems: ₹75,184 per kW against ₹71,184 for 2 kW. Why the subsidy favours 1–2 kW

The PM Surya Ghar subsidy for a home is ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW, ₹18,000 for the third kW, and it stops at ₹78,000 at 3 kW. So the first two kilowatts get the most support. On the calculator's prices the subsidy covers about 40% of a 1 kW system and 42% of a 2 kW system, against 37% at 3 kW and less above that. Net cost per kW is lowest at exactly 2 kW:

What 1 kW and 2 kW can and cannot do

  • Offset, not run. An on-grid system does not power particular appliances. It feeds your home's wiring; whatever you are using draws on solar first and the grid for the rest, and surplus flows out through the net meter. Over a year, 1 kW in Delhi offsets about 1,570 units and 2 kW about 3,140.
  • No backup in a power cut. Grid-tied inverters shut down when the grid fails, for the safety of line workers. If outages matter, look at a hybrid system with a battery, which costs considerably more.
  • Seasons. Output is higher in March–May and lower in the monsoon and in winter haze. The figures here are yearly averages.

Connection and approvals

  • Sanctioned load. National net-metering rules allow a rooftop system up to your sanctioned load. Check the load on your bill; if it is below the size you want, apply for an enhancement first.
  • Single-phase is fine. Inverters at 1–2 kW are single-phase units, so these sizes work on an ordinary home connection.
  • No feasibility study. Since the 2024 amendment to the consumer rules, rooftop systems up to 10 kW need no technical feasibility study by the DISCOM.
  • Subsidy conditions. Apply on the national PM Surya Ghar portal, install through a registered vendor and use DCR panels. The subsidy is paid into your bank account after the system is commissioned and inspected.

Paying for a small system

Scheme loans for systems up to 3 kW are collateral-free, at a rate linked to the RBI repo rate. At 1–2 kW the amount is small, and the subsidy returns 40% or more of the calculator price once the system is commissioned, so paying upfront is often practical. The solar loan and EMI guide covers the loan terms and lets you compare an EMI with your monthly saving.

Before you sign for 1 kW or 2 kW

  1. Add up 12 months of units and divide by 30 and by your state's peak sun hours (table above) to confirm the size.
  2. Check your sanctioned load on the bill.
  3. Get at least three quotes for the same kW, panel type (DCR for the subsidy), inverter and structure, and compare the price per kW.
  4. Ask for the expected units per year in writing and compare it with the figures here.
  5. Confirm who files the subsidy application and the net-meter paperwork, and what after-sales service is included.

Frequently asked questions

What is the price of a 1 kW solar system in India?
Published 2026 price guides quote basic 1 kW on-grid systems from about ₹45,000–65,000 before subsidy, with full-service packages up to about ₹1.2–1.3 lakh. The calculator's benchmark estimate is ₹75,184 including the net meter. After the ₹30,000 PM Surya Ghar subsidy, the calculator's net cost is ₹45,184.
What is the price of a 2 kW solar system after subsidy?
Published 2026 price guides quote 2 kW on-grid systems from about ₹85,000–1.25 lakh before subsidy, with full-service packages up to about ₹1.8–1.9 lakh. The calculator's benchmark estimate is ₹1,42,368. The PM Surya Ghar subsidy for 2 kW is ₹60,000, so the calculator's net cost is ₹82,368, or ₹41,184 per kW, the lowest per kW of any home size.
How many units does a 2 kW solar system generate per day?
About 7–10 units a day averaged over the year, depending on the state: around 8.6 units a day (about 260 a month, 3,139 a year) in Delhi or Maharashtra, 7.0 in Kerala and 10.1 in Rajasthan. A 1 kW system makes half that, about 4.3 units a day in Delhi.
How much subsidy is available for 1 kW and 2 kW solar?
The central PM Surya Ghar subsidy is ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW, so ₹30,000 for a 1 kW system and ₹60,000 for 2 kW. Fractional sizes are pro-rata, so 1.5 kW gets ₹45,000. The third kW adds ₹18,000, and the subsidy stops at ₹78,000 for 3 kW and above.
