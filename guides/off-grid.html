Off-Grid Solar in India: Battery Size, Cost & Payback

Full energy independence with battery storage — works through blackouts, needs no DISCOM. But batteries are expensive, they wear out, and there's no subsidy. Here's the honest picture, with battery sizes, 2026 prices and the calculator's own numbers.

Best for: Rural homes, farmhouses, areas with 4+ hours of daily outage, or anyone who cannot tolerate a power cut.
Quick answer: a home using 150 units a month needs about 1.3 kW of panels and an 8.8 kWh lithium (LFP) battery to run fully off-grid for 1.5 days without sun. In the calculator (Delhi) that costs ₹3,18,975 with no subsidy and pays back in about 14 years, counting diesel you no longer burn. The same home on-grid pays ₹45,184 after subsidy for a 1.0 kW system with a 5-year payback. Off-grid is worth it where the grid is absent or unreliable, not as a way to save money.

What "off-grid" means

An off-grid system is fully self-contained. Panels charge a battery bank during the day; the battery powers your home at night and during outages. You're either not connected to the DISCOM at all, or you keep the grid connection purely as emergency backup.

How big a battery do you need?

Off-grid batteries are sized for days, not hours: the bank has to carry your whole home through cloudy days with no grid to fall back on.

Battery size (kWh) = daily use (units) × days of autonomy ÷ usable depth of discharge
Daily use = monthly units ÷ 30. Usable depth of discharge is about 85% for lithium iron phosphate (LFP), 90% for premium lithium and 50% for lead-acid tubular — the figures the calculator uses. A home using 150 units a month (5 units a day) needs 5 × 1.5 ÷ 0.85 = 8.8 kWh of LFP for 1.5 days, or 15 kWh of lead-acid.

Days of autonomy is the main choice you make. 1.5 days is the practical minimum for most of India; 2 days is monsoon-safe, and remote or critical sites use 3. Every extra day of autonomy adds another full day of your use to the battery. Why off-grid systems are oversized

You size an off-grid system for your worst day, not your average. Two consecutive cloudy days with no grid means your battery must carry the entire load, and the panels then have to refill it while still running the house. This is why off-grid systems are typically 30–50% larger than an equivalent on-grid system for the same home; the calculator adds 35% to the panel size to cover cloudy days and battery charging losses. Monsoon-heavy regions like Kerala and coastal Karnataka need even more headroom — plan for 2–3 days of battery autonomy.

Plan for battery replacement

Panels last 25 years; batteries don't. With lead-acid, budget for a full battery swap around every 5 years — four replacements over a 25-year system life. This "lifetime battery cost" is the single biggest hidden number in off-grid, and our off-grid solar calculator spells it out for you. Lead-acid looks cheapest on day one but costs the most over the life of the system, which is why LFP is usually the better buy. The calculator counts replacements as 25 ÷ battery life, rounded down, minus one, so it shows none for a 15-year battery; if you keep the system for the full 25 years, expect to replace that one too.

How the money works

You're not selling anything. Your return is the electricity bill you avoid entirely, plus the diesel generator you stop running. With diesel at about ₹95 a litre (Delhi, November 2026) and a generator making roughly 3–4 units per litre, fuel alone costs about ₹24–32 per unit, and more when a generator runs well below its rated load, as small home sets usually do. In areas with 6+ hours of daily load-shedding, the diesel fuel savings alone often justify the system.

The calculator is deliberately cautious here: it assumes a generator was covering 30% of your load at ₹18 per unit. For the 150-unit Delhi home that is ₹9,855 a year on top of the ₹12,775 bill saving. Without any diesel saving, the payback stretches from about 14 years to 25 — roughly the life of the panels — so off-grid only makes financial sense when it replaces a generator or an unreliable supply.

Where off-grid wins

  • Works through blackouts — the single biggest reason people go off-grid
  • No DISCOM paperwork — no net-metering application, no approval wait
  • Essential for remote sites — farmhouses, hill stations, telecom towers, border areas
  • Replaces diesel generators, which now cost roughly ₹24–32 per unit in fuel alone

The catches

  • No subsidy. PM Surya Ghar is only for grid-connected rooftop systems with net metering, so off-grid residential systems get nothing.
  • Batteries die. Factor replacement into your true cost of ownership.
  • No export income. Surplus on sunny days is wasted once the battery is full.
  • Higher maintenance than on-grid — a strong local service network matters more than a big brand name.

Off-grid solar FAQ

How much does an off-grid solar system cost in India?
An October 2026 price survey put complete off-grid systems at about ₹2.8–3.4 lakh for 3 kW with a tubular lead-acid battery and ₹3.4–4.1 lakh with an LFP lithium battery, and ₹4.5–5.5 lakh or ₹5.4–6.5 lakh for 5 kW. There is no subsidy. The price depends mostly on how many kWh of battery is included: the calculator, which sizes the battery for 1.5 full days, puts a Delhi home using 300 units a month at 2.5 kW with 17.6 kWh of LFP for ₹6,32,368.
What size battery do I need for an off-grid solar system?
Multiply your daily use by the days of autonomy you want and divide by the battery's usable depth of discharge. A home using 150 units a month uses 5 units a day, so 1.5 days needs 5 × 1.5 ÷ 0.85 = 8.8 kWh of LFP, or 15 kWh of lead-acid at 50% depth. For 300 units a month it is 17.6 kWh of LFP.
Lead-acid or lithium battery for off-grid solar?
Lithium (LFP) for most homes. Lead-acid is cheaper upfront but only half of it is usable and it lasts about 5 years. For a 150-unit home with 1.5 days of autonomy, the calculator's lead-acid bank costs ₹1,80,000 but about ₹9,00,000 over 25 years with four replacements, against ₹2,46,400 upfront and about ₹4,92,800 over 25 years for LFP.
Can I get any subsidy for off-grid?
Not the residential PM Surya Ghar subsidy — that requires grid connection and net metering. Certain state or sector-specific schemes (agriculture, remote electrification) may apply in some cases, but standard residential off-grid does not qualify.
What's the difference from hybrid?
A hybrid system stays grid-connected and keeps a small battery only for essentials during outages. Off-grid disconnects entirely and must carry your full load on batteries. Hybrid is cheaper and can still claim subsidy on the solar portion; off-grid is for places where the grid is absent or hopeless.
How many days of battery backup do I need?
1.5 days is the practical minimum for most of India. Monsoon-heavy or remote/critical sites should use 2–3 days. More autonomy means a bigger, costlier battery bank, so it's a direct trade-off against budget.
