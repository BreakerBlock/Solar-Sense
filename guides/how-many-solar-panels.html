How Many Solar Panels Do I Need?

The number of solar panels you need comes down to three things: how many units of electricity you use, how much sunshine your state gets, and the wattage of the panels you buy. Here is the exact method, with tables for typical Indian homes.

Quick answer: at 5 peak sun hours (the Indian average), our rooftop solar calculator sizes about 1 kW for every 150 units you use a month. A home using 300 units a month needs about 2 kW, which is 4 panels of 550 W or 5 panels of 400 W, and about 160–200 sq ft of shadow-free roof.

Step 1: Find your monthly units

Your electricity bill shows the units (kWh) you used in the billing period. Use the average of the last 12 months if you can, because summer bills with air-conditioning can be twice the winter ones. If you only know the rupee amount, divide it by your tariff: a ₹3,000 bill at ₹7 per unit is about 430 units. Your state's electricity tariff is listed on our tariff pages.

Step 2: Turn units into system size (kW)

Divide your daily use by your state's peak sun hours:

System size (kW) = monthly units ÷ 30 ÷ peak sun hours

Peak sun hours are the number of hours a day of full-strength sunlight your location gets on average. They range from about 4.5 in Kerala and West Bengal to 6.5 in Rajasthan. For 300 units a month at 5 peak sun hours: 300 ÷ 30 = 10 units a day, and 10 ÷ 5 = 2 kW. This is exactly how the rooftop solar calculator sizes an on-grid system, rounded up to the next 0.1 kW.

Real panels never deliver their full rating. Heat, dust, wiring and the inverter cost about 22%, so each kW produces roughly 4 units a day (about 1,400 a year) at 5 peak sun hours. Our solar generation per kW guide lists the figure for every state. Sized this way, the system covers around 80% of your annual use, which keeps most of the output for your own consumption rather than export at the lower buyback rate. If you want to offset close to 100% of your units, divide by 0.78 as well: 300 units a month then needs about 2.6 kW.

Step 3: Turn kW into a number of panels

Number of panels = system size in watts ÷ panel wattage, rounded up

Rooftop panels sold in India today are mostly in the 400–450 W and 500–550 W ranges, with larger 560–600 W+ modules used on bigger roofs. A 2 kW system is 2,000 W, so it needs 2,000 ÷ 550 = 3.6, rounded up to 4 panels of 550 W, or 2,000 ÷ 400 = 5 panels of 400 W.

Because you can only buy whole panels, the installed capacity usually ends up slightly above the target. Four 550 W panels are 2.2 kWp, not 2 kWp. That is normal, and the PM Surya Ghar subsidy is worked out on the rated capacity of the panels actually installed. To claim the subsidy, the panels must also be domestically manufactured (DCR) modules installed by a registered vendor. See the subsidy guide for the slabs.

How much roof space you need

Mono PERC panels take about 55 sq ft per kW (about 50 sq ft with TOPCon and 67 sq ft with polycrystalline). Including walkways, edge clearance and gaps that keep rows from shading each other, plan for 80–100 sq ft of shadow-free roof per kW. A 3 kW home system therefore needs about 240–300 sq ft. If space is tight, higher-efficiency panels fit more capacity on the same roof. Our panel types guide compares them.

What else changes the panel count

  • Off-grid systems need more panels. Without the grid to fall back on, the calculator oversizes off-grid systems by 35% to cover cloudy days and battery charging losses. See the off-grid solar guide.
  • Planned loads. If you are adding an EV, more air-conditioners or an induction cooktop, size for next year's bill, not last year's.
  • Sanctioned load. Net metering is allowed up to your sanctioned (connected) load, so a system much bigger than your connection may need a load upgrade first. Net metering explained covers the rules.
  • The subsidy stops growing at 3 kW. The central subsidy is capped at ₹78,000 for any system of 3 kW or more, so size to your use, not to the subsidy.

Frequently asked questions

How many solar panels do I need for a 3 kW system?
Six panels of 550 W (3.3 kWp) or eight panels of 400 W (3.2 kWp). Panel counts are always rounded up, so the installed capacity ends up slightly above 3 kW. The central subsidy for anything from 3 kW upwards is the same ₹78,000.
How many units does a 1 kW solar system produce?
About 4 units a day, or roughly 1,400 units a year, at 5 peak sun hours after real-world losses. In Rajasthan, with 6.5 peak sun hours, it is closer to 1,850 units a year. In Kerala or West Bengal, with 4.5 hours, it is about 1,280.
How many solar panels do I need for 300 units a month?
About 2 kW at 5 peak sun hours, which is 4 panels of 550 W or 5 panels of 400 W. In a lower-sunshine state such as Kerala it is about 2.3 kW (5 panels of 550 W). If you want to offset all 300 units rather than about 80% of them, plan for about 2.6 kW.
Is it better to buy fewer high-wattage panels?
For the same total kW, the electricity produced is about the same. Higher-wattage panels mean fewer modules, fewer connections and slightly less mounting work, and they help on small roofs. The choice usually comes down to price per watt, roof shape and whether the panel model is approved for the subsidy.
