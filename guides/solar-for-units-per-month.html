Solar for 200, 300 or 500 Units a Month: Size, Cost and Savings

Your electricity bill already tells you what size of rooftop solar to buy: the number that matters is the units (kWh) you use each month. This guide turns 100 to 1,000 units a month into a system size, a price before and after the PM Surya Ghar subsidy, a monthly saving and a payback period, then shows how the answer for 300 units changes from state to state.

Quick answer: in a state with about 5.5 peak sun hours, such as Delhi or Maharashtra, a home using 200 units a month needs about 1.3 kW, 300 units about 1.9 kW and 500 units about 3.1 kW. After the subsidy those systems cost roughly ₹56,000, ₹79,000 and ₹1.38 lakh at the calculator's benchmark price, save about ₹980, ₹1,440 and ₹2,340 a month in Delhi, and pay back in under 5 years. In less sunny states you need up to about 20% more kW for the same units.

How monthly units become a system size

The solar calculator sizes an on-grid system with one rule: monthly units ÷ 30 ÷ peak sun hours, rounded up to the next 0.1 kW. A Delhi home using 300 units a month uses 10 units a day; at 5.5 peak sun hours that is 10 ÷ 5.5 = 1.82, so 1.9 kW.

Panels never deliver their full rating all day, so the calculator applies a 0.78 performance ratio to generation. A system sized this way therefore makes about 78–80% of the units you use over a year, which keeps it from over-producing units that would mostly be exported at the low buyback rate. If you want solar to cover close to 100% of your units, choose about a quarter more kW; see how many solar panels you need for that version of the sizing and the panel counts.

This is in line with the scheme's own guidance: PM Surya Ghar material suggests 1–2 kW for homes using up to 150 units a month, 2–3 kW for 150–300 units, and above 3 kW for more than 300 units.

Three patterns show up. First, payback is shortest, about 4.6 years, between 250 and 300 units. Those homes land on 1.6–1.9 kW, where the subsidy pays ₹30,000 a kW. What about "300 units of free electricity"?

PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana is often described as giving homes up to 300 units of free electricity a month. That is not a separate free allowance from your DISCOM. It means a rooftop system of the right size can generate about 300 units a month, which then offsets the units you would otherwise buy. How much solar makes 300 units a month depends on sunshine:

So roughly 2–3 kW generates 300 units a month across most of India, all inside the range where the subsidy is ₹60,000–78,000. Generation is an average: summer months produce more and monsoon or winter-haze months less, so even a 300-unit system will not zero every bill. Under net metering, surplus units from sunny months can be credited against later bills; the generation guide shows how output swings through the year.

Read your units from the bill, not the rupees

  • Use a year of bills. Units in May and June can be twice those in January. Add 12 months and divide by 12, or the system will be sized for your worst month.
  • Use units, not the amount. Bills include fixed charges, duties and slab rates. If you only know the amount, divide it by your average tariff, as the calculator's bill mode does, but expect the answer to be rougher.
  • Add what is coming. A 1.5 ton AC run 8 hours a day adds roughly 200–430 units a month in summer, depending on its type, and an EV charged at home adds more; see solar for AC. It is cheaper to size for it now than to add panels later.
  • Check your sanctioned load. National net-metering rules allow rooftop solar up to your sanctioned load, so a home on a 2 kW connection that needs 3.1 kW may first need a load enhancement.

Frequently asked questions

How many kW of solar do I need for 300 units a month?
About 1.9 kW in a state with 5.5 peak sun hours such as Delhi or Maharashtra, 2 kW at 5 sun hours, 2.3 kW in Kerala or West Bengal and 1.6 kW in Rajasthan. That size makes about 78–80% of your units over a year. To generate the full 300 units a month you need about 2–3 kW, for example 2.4 kW in Delhi.
How much does a solar system for 300 units a month cost?
In Delhi the calculator sizes 1.9 kW at ₹1,35,650 before subsidy. The PM Surya Ghar subsidy is ₹57,000 at that size, so the net cost is ₹78,650, saving about ₹1,440 a month with a payback of about 4.6 years. Across the 22 states the net cost is about ₹67,000–97,000 and payback 4.4–8.8 years.
How much solar do I need for 500 units a month?
About 3.1 kW at 5.5 peak sun hours, or 3.4–3.7 kW in less sunny states. In Delhi the calculator puts it at ₹2,16,270 before subsidy and ₹1,38,270 after the full ₹78,000 subsidy, saving about ₹2,340 a month with a payback of about 4.9 years.
Does PM Surya Ghar give 300 units of electricity free?
Not as a separate free allowance. The scheme subsidises a rooftop solar system, and a system of about 2–3 kW generates around 300 units a month, which offsets units you would otherwise buy from the DISCOM. The subsidy is ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third, capped at ₹78,000.
