Solar Loan and EMI in India (2026): PM Surya Ghar Loans Explained

You pay the full price of a rooftop system before the PM Surya Ghar subsidy reaches your account, which is why many households take a solar loan. Here are the scheme's loan terms, what the EMI works out to, how it compares with what the panels save you each month, and how to use the subsidy to close the loan years early.

Quick answer: for systems up to 3 kW, public sector banks give collateral-free solar loans of up to ₹2 lakh at a rate linked to the RBI repo rate (repo + 0.5%, about 6–7%), for up to 10 years with a 10% down payment. A typical 3 kW system in Delhi (₹2.1 lakh before subsidy) needs a loan of about ₹1.89 lakh, an EMI of about ₹2,100–2,200 a month over 10 years, against average savings of about ₹2,270 a month. Put the ₹78,000 subsidy towards the loan when it arrives and it closes in about 5 years.

PM Surya Ghar solar loan terms

Under the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana, 12 public sector banks lend for residential grid-connected rooftop systems. Applications go through the government's Jan Samarth portal and the money is paid directly to your installer. The terms below are the scheme's headline terms as published by the government and reported by banks; individual banks can differ in detail.

Systems up to 3 kWSystems above 3 kW (up to 10 kW)
Loan amountUp to ₹2 lakhUp to about ₹6 lakh
Interest rateRepo rate + 0.5% (floating)Higher, linked to the bank's home-loan rate (quoted at roughly 8–10%)
Your down payment (margin)10% of system cost20% of system cost
TenureUp to 10 years (120 months), including a moratorium of up to 6 months
CollateralNoneUsually the solar system itself is hypothecated to the bank, not your property
PrepaymentNo prepayment penalty under the scheme

Why "about 6–7%". The rate for loans up to ₹2 lakh moves with the RBI repo rate. When the government described the scheme in early 2025 the repo rate was 6.25% and the loan rate 6.75% ("around 7%"). The RBI cut the repo rate to 5.25% during 2025 and raised it to 5.50% on 7 October 2026, so the same formula now gives about 6%. Banks reset floating rates on their own schedules, and the rate can change again over a 10-year loan, so check the rate on your sanction letter. A credit score of around 680 or more helps, though first-time borrowers with no credit history can also apply, and PAN is optional for loans up to 3 kW.

Monthly EMI: ₹2,094

The EMI uses the standard reducing-balance formula: EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1), where P is the loan, r the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n the number of months. It assumes repayment starts in the first month; if you take a moratorium, interest still builds up during it, so the EMI afterwards is a little higher. For your system's cost and subsidy, use the rooftop solar calculator first.

EMI vs monthly savings: worked examples

The useful question is not just what the EMI is, but whether the panels save more than it each month. These are the calculator's estimates for a Delhi home on grid-connected solar, with the loan taken on the maximum share the scheme allows (90% up to 3 kW, 80% above):

Inputs: Delhi, on-grid, Mono PERC panels, ₹7/kWh tariff and ₹3.50/kWh buyback (the calculator's Delhi defaults), monthly use of 330, 495, 660 and 825 units. The average saving is the calculator's first-year benefit (bill savings plus export credit) divided by 12. The 6% rate is the repo + 0.5% formula at today's repo rate; 9% is a mid-range figure for the larger loans.

In every case the EMI is a little below the average monthly saving even before the subsidy arrives, so the system roughly pays its own instalments from the start. Two honest caveats: savings are seasonal (higher in sunny, high-use months, lower in the monsoon and winter) while the EMI is fixed, so some months you will be slightly out of pocket; and in states with lower tariffs or less sun the saving is smaller, so the EMI can exceed it. Check your own state's figure in the solar EMI and payback calculator before you borrow.

Use the subsidy to prepay the loan

The central subsidy is credited to your bank account after the system is commissioned and inspected (see how the PM Surya Ghar subsidy is paid). Since there is no prepayment penalty, putting it straight into the loan is usually the best use of it. For the 3 kW example (₹1,88,597 at 6%, ₹78,000 prepaid in month 3), there are two ways to do it:

OptionMonthly EMILoan closes inTotal interest
No prepayment₹2,094120 months₹62,661
Prepay subsidy, keep the EMI₹2,09463 months (about 5¼ years)₹19,726
Prepay subsidy, keep the tenure₹1,212 from month 4120 months₹37,444

Keeping the EMI saves the most interest. Keeping the tenure gives you an instalment well below the monthly saving, so the system puts money in your pocket from month 4. For the 2 kW example, prepaying its ₹60,000 subsidy closes the loan in 56 months; for 5 kW at 9%, the ₹78,000 subsidy closes it in 75 months.

Loan or cash?

Paying cash, the 3 kW Delhi system pays for itself in about 4.8 years (₹1,31,552 net cost ÷ ₹27,195 first-year benefit). On the loan, with the subsidy prepaid and the EMI kept, you also pay about ₹19,700 in interest, which adds roughly 0.7 of a year, so the break-even moves to about 5.6 years. Over a panel life of 25 years or more, that difference is small, and the loan means you need only about ₹21,000 upfront instead of ₹2.1 lakh.

If you have the cash, compare the loan rate with what that money earns elsewhere: borrowing at about 6% while your savings earn more can make sense; borrowing at 9–10% for a larger system usually costs more than it earns. The calculator itself does not include loan interest in its payback figure, so add it yourself using the table above.

How to get a PM Surya Ghar solar loan

  1. Register on the national portal (pmsuryaghar.gov.in) with your electricity consumer number and mobile number, and apply for rooftop solar.
  2. Choose a registered vendor and get a quote for the system size you need. Make sure the quote uses DCR (domestically made cell) panels, which the subsidy requires.
  3. Apply for the loan through the Jan Samarth portal (linked from the national portal), choosing a bank. Keep your KYC, latest electricity bill, the vendor quote and bank statements ready; PAN is needed above 3 kW.
  4. Sanction and disbursement. After you pay your down payment, the bank pays the loan amount to the vendor.
  5. Installation and net meter. The vendor installs the system and the DISCOM inspects it and fits the net meter.
  6. Subsidy and prepayment. The subsidy is credited to your bank account. Prepay it into the loan and tell the bank whether to reduce the EMI or the tenure.

What to check before you sign

  • Fixed or floating. Most scheme loans are floating, so the EMI or tenure changes when the repo rate does. Ask how often the bank resets it.
  • Moratorium. A moratorium delays the first EMI but not the interest. If you don't need it, start repaying straight away.
  • Charges. Ask for processing fees, documentation charges and insurance in writing; for scheme loans up to ₹2 lakh, processing fees are usually waived.
  • The quote behind the loan. The loan amount is based on the vendor's quote, so compare at least three quotes first (our solar panel cost guide has 2026 price ranges per kW).
  • Size to your use, not to the loan limit. A system bigger than you need exports more units at the low buyback rate, so the extra kW often save less than their EMI. See how many solar panels you need.

Off-grid, hybrid and commercial systems

The scheme's subsidy and its low-rate loan are for residential, grid-connected rooftop systems. An off-grid system with batteries gets no subsidy, so it has no subsidy to prepay with, and any loan for it is at the bank's normal rates for personal or home-improvement lending. Businesses finance rooftop solar with business loans instead and claim accelerated depreciation; see the commercial rooftop solar guide.

Frequently asked questions

What is the interest rate on a PM Surya Ghar solar loan?
For loans up to ₹2 lakh (systems up to 3 kW), the rate is linked to the RBI repo rate at repo + 0.5%. That was 6.75% when the repo rate was 6.25%, and works out to about 6% at the 5.50% repo rate set on 7 October 2026. Larger loans for systems above 3 kW are charged at higher rates linked to the bank's home-loan rate, roughly 8–10%. Check the rate on your sanction letter, as banks reset floating rates on their own schedule.
What is the EMI for a 3 kW solar system?
A 3 kW grid-connected system costing about ₹2.1 lakh needs a loan of about ₹1.89 lakh with a 10% down payment. Over 10 years that is about ₹2,094 a month at 6% or ₹2,190 at 7%. If you prepay the ₹78,000 subsidy when it arrives and keep the same EMI, the loan closes in about 5 years.
Do I need collateral for a solar loan?
Not for scheme loans up to ₹2 lakh, which cover systems up to 3 kW. For larger loans banks usually take the solar system itself as security (hypothecation) rather than your house or other property.
Can I use the PM Surya Ghar subsidy to repay my solar loan?
Yes. The subsidy is credited to your own bank account after commissioning, and scheme loans have no prepayment penalty, so you can pay it into the loan. Keeping the same EMI closes a 3 kW loan in about 63 months instead of 120 and cuts the interest from about ₹62,700 to about ₹19,700; keeping the same tenure lowers the EMI from ₹2,094 to about ₹1,212.
