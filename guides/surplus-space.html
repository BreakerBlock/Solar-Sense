Earning From Surplus Space

If you have more space than you need power — a big terrace, a warehouse roof, a shed, or a small idle plot — you can fill it with solar and sell what you don't use. Here's how the economics actually work, and the constraint that trips most people up.

Best for: Homeowners with large terraces, small businesses with warehouse roofs, or anyone sitting on an idle plot near an existing electricity connection.

The core idea

Your own consumption might need 3 kWp. But your roof has room for 12 kWp. Those extra 9 kWp generate power you'll never use yourself — so it goes to the grid, and the DISCOM pays you for it. Instead of sizing solar to your bill, you size it to your space.

What you actually earn

Self-consumed units are worth your full retail tariff (₹5–7.5/kWh) because you avoid buying them. Exported units earn the buyback rate (₹2–3.5/kWh), which is always lower. So the economics are strongest when you use as much as you can and sell the remainder.

The single highest-return move is shifting load into daylight hours — charging an EV during the day, running the washing machine and dishwasher at noon, pre-cooling the house before evening, heating water electrically. Every unit you shift from "export at ₹2.50" to "self-use at ₹7" is worth far more than adding another panel.

The three real constraints

1. Sanctioned load cap. Most states cap your rooftop system at 100–150% of your sanctioned load. If your connection is 5 kW, you may not be allowed to install 15 kWp without upgrading the connection first. Check this before anything else — it kills more projects than cost does.

2. Subsidy ceiling. PM Surya Ghar caps at ₹78,000 for 3 kW and tapers to ₹1,41,000 at 10 kW. Beyond 10 kWp there is no residential subsidy at all.

3. Buyback isn't cash everywhere. Some DISCOMs credit units against future bills rather than paying cash. If you export far more than you consume, those credits may expire unused at the financial year end.

Realistic returns

A 10 kWp rooftop in a ₹7/kWh state with 50% self-consumption returns roughly 14–16% annually and pays back in 5–6 years. Push self-consumption higher and it improves sharply. The yield also rises every year as grid tariffs climb ~3% annually while your cost stays fixed — and because avoided electricity bills aren't income, the return is effectively tax-free. Frequently asked questions

How do I find my sanctioned load?
It's printed on your electricity bill, usually in kW. This number, times your state's cap (100–150%), sets the largest system your DISCOM will approve without a connection upgrade.
Is this the same as a solar power plant?
No. This is about filling spare space you already have — a terrace, a shed roof, a small plot — not building a utility-scale plant with land acquisition, PPAs, and grid connectivity engineering. It stays close to a normal rooftop installation, just larger.
Can I claim depreciation benefits?
If the system is on a commercial connection and you have business income, you can typically claim 40% accelerated depreciation in year one, which is a meaningful tax benefit. This does not apply to residential systems. Consult a tax advisor for your situation.
