5 kW Solar System for Home in India: Price, Units, Subsidy and Payback

A 5 kW rooftop system is the usual choice for a larger home with two or three air conditioners, or a family whose bill regularly runs past 700 units a month. Here is what a 5 kW on-grid system costs in 2026, how many units it makes in each state, what the PM Surya Ghar subsidy covers, how long it takes to pay back, and what to check on your connection before you sign.

Quick answer: a 5 kW system makes about 21 units a day, or ~650 units a month in Delhi (18–25 a day across states), and suits homes using roughly 675–975 units a month depending on how sunny the state is. 2026 quotes mostly run ₹2.5–3.5 lakh before subsidy. The home subsidy is ₹78,000, the same as for 3 kW. In Delhi the calculator puts the payback at 5.9 years, or about 4.9 years on a ₹3 lakh quote. It needs about 400–500 sq ft of shadow-free roof and a sanctioned load of about 5 kW.

5 kW solar price in 2026

Price guides published in 2026 mostly put a complete 5 kW on-grid home system at about ₹2.5–3.5 lakh before subsidy, roughly ₹50,000–70,000 per kW. DCR panels (needed for the subsidy), an elevated structure, premium modules or a full-service package push quotes higher, and the solar panel cost guide uses a wider ₹2.75–4.25 lakh band to cover those.

The calculator uses ₹65,000 per kW for every on-grid system plus ₹8,000 for the net meter, which gives ₹3,43,920 for 5 kW with Mono PERC panels. With TOPCon panels it gives ₹3,63,030; they make the same units in the calculator but need only about 245 sq ft instead of 276, so they help on a tight roof (payback 6.3 years in Delhi). See solar panel types compared.

The PM Surya Ghar subsidy for a home is ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kW, and it stops at ₹78,000 at 3 kW. A 5 kW system gets the same ₹78,000, about 23% of the calculator's price, or 22–31% of a ₹2.5–3.5 lakh quote. So the net cost is about ₹1.7–2.7 lakh, and the extra 2 kW above 3 kW is paid for in full. Some states add a top-up of their own; check your state's solar portal, as the calculator does not include state top-ups.

Payback lengthens slowly with size, so a 5 kW system that matches your use is still a sound buy. What hurts is oversizing: if your home uses about 495 units a month and you install 5 kW, most of the extra ~3,100 units a year are exported at the buyback rate, and the extra 2 kW takes roughly 12 years to pay back in Delhi. 5 kW with battery backup

A 5 kW on-grid system shuts down during a power cut, as all grid-tied systems must for line-worker safety. Adding a battery makes it a hybrid. The calculator's hybrid option sizes an 11.3 kWh lithium (LFP) bank for 825 units a month, which adds about ₹3,16,400 and stretches the Delhi payback from 5.9 to about 12.9 years, because the battery does not add savings where the grid is reliable. A smaller bank that runs only lights, fans, the fridge and Wi-Fi costs much less. The hybrid solar guide explains when backup is worth paying for.

Connection, sanctioned load and approvals

  • Sanctioned load. National net-metering rules allow a rooftop system up to your sanctioned load, so a home on a 3 kW connection usually has to apply for a load enhancement to 5 kW first, with a one-time charge and often slightly higher fixed charges. Some states have relaxed this for homes; check your DISCOM's current rules.
  • Single-phase or three-phase. Many 5 kW home systems run on a single-phase connection with a single-phase inverter, but the limit for solar on single-phase supply is set by each state. Andhra Pradesh, for example, allowed only 3 kW on single-phase until its regulator raised the limit to 5 kW (up to the sanctioned load) in August 2026, and Rajasthan's DISCOMs now balance phases at the transformer before releasing single-phase rooftop connections. Ask your DISCOM before you sign.
  • Feasibility. Rooftop systems up to 10 kW need no technical feasibility study by the DISCOM under the 2024 amendment to the consumer rules, so a 5 kW application is mostly paperwork.
  • Subsidy conditions. To claim the ₹78,000 you apply on the national PM Surya Ghar portal, use a registered vendor and DCR (made-in-India cell) panels. The subsidy goes to one residential connection and does not grow for a bigger system.

Roof space and panels

Five kW of Mono PERC panels covers about 276 sq ft: 13 panels of 400 W, or 10 of 550 W. Allow 400–500 sq ft of shadow-free roof once walkways and row spacing are added, more if water tanks, stair rooms or parapets cast shade. On a smaller terrace, higher-efficiency panels or an elevated structure can help. The panel count guide explains how to work out panels and space for any size.

Paying for it with a loan

Collateral-free PM Surya Ghar loans at a repo-linked rate cover systems up to 3 kW; for 5 kW, loans are larger and priced nearer home-loan rates, roughly 8–10%. As an illustration, a loan of ₹2,75,136 (80% of the calculator price) over 10 years at 9% is about ₹3,485 a month, against an average Delhi saving of about ₹3,759 a month. Try your own amount, rate and tenure in the solar loan and EMI guide.

Before you sign for 5 kW

  1. Add up 12 months of units and check them against the table above, so you are not paying for an extra 1–2 kW you will mostly export.
  2. Check your sanctioned load and phase on the bill, and ask the DISCOM whether 5 kW is allowed on your connection.
  3. Get at least three quotes for the same kW, panel type (DCR or not), inverter and structure height, and compare the price per kW.
  4. Ask for the expected units per year in writing; for 5 kW it should be close to the yearly figure for your state above.
  5. Confirm who files the net meter and subsidy applications, and what the warranty and after-sales service cover.

Frequently asked questions

What is the price of a 5 kW solar system in India?
Price guides published in 2026 mostly put a complete 5 kW on-grid home system at about ₹2.5–3.5 lakh before subsidy; premium panels, elevated structures and full-service packages cost more. The calculator's benchmark estimate is ₹3,43,920 with Mono PERC panels. After the ₹78,000 PM Surya Ghar subsidy, the net cost is about ₹1.7–2.7 lakh.
How many units does a 5 kW solar system generate per day?
About 18–25 units a day averaged over the year, depending on the state: around 21.4 units a day (about 650 a month, 7,811 a year) in Delhi or Maharashtra, 17.6 in Kerala and West Bengal, and 25.4 in Rajasthan. Summer months produce more and monsoon or winter-haze months less.
How much subsidy is available on a 5 kW solar system?
For a home, the central PM Surya Ghar subsidy is ₹78,000, the same as for a 3 kW system, because it covers only the first 3 kW: ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third. Some states add a top-up of their own. Housing societies get a different rate for common facilities.
Can a 5 kW solar system run on a single-phase connection?
Often yes, but it depends on your state. Many 5 kW home systems use a single-phase inverter, while the limit for rooftop solar on single-phase supply is set by each state's rules; Andhra Pradesh, for example, raised its limit from 3 kW to 5 kW in August 2026. You will also normally need a sanctioned load of about 5 kW, so check your bill and ask your DISCOM before you sign.
