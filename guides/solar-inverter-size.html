Solar Inverter Size: What kW Inverter Do You Need for Your Panels?

Panels are sold in kW of DC capacity and inverters in kW of AC output, and the two do not have to be exactly equal. This guide shows how to match an inverter to your panels, what net metering and your sanctioned load allow, how off-grid and hybrid inverters are sized differently, and what an inverter costs in 2026, with a size table worked out from the SolarSense solar calculator.

Quick answer: for an on-grid home, pick an inverter whose AC rating is about the same as your panel capacity, usually within 0.9–1.1 times it. A 3 kW array gets a 3 kW inverter, 5 kW of panels a 5 kW inverter. The inverter's AC rating should not be more than your sanctioned load, and a March 2026 MNRE advisory asks DISCOMs to accept up to 10% more panel (DC) capacity than the system you applied for. Off-grid and hybrid inverters are sized to the loads you run at the same time, not just to the panels.

Panel kW vs inverter kW

Your panels produce direct current (DC); the inverter turns it into the alternating current (AC) your home and the grid use. The DC:AC ratio is panel capacity divided by inverter rating: 3.3 kW of panels on a 3 kW inverter is a ratio of 1.1.

Panels rarely deliver their full rated output in real conditions. Heat, dust, wiring and inverter losses are why the calculator uses a 0.78 performance ratio (see solar generation per kW). That is why installers often put slightly more panel capacity than inverter capacity: the inverter runs closer to its rating for more of the day. On a very clear, cool day the panels can briefly produce more than the inverter can pass on, and the extra is lost. This is called clipping.

  • Typical ratios. 2026 industry guides put Indian home rooftops at roughly 1.1–1.25. They estimate clipping at around 1–2% of yearly output or less in that range, rising quickly above about 1.3.
  • Ratio below 1. An inverter a little larger than the array (say 2.8 kW of panels on a 3 kW inverter) is fine. It does not clip, and it leaves room to add a panel later if your sanctioned load allows.
  • The datasheet limit. Every inverter has a maximum DC input power and voltage. The array must stay inside it however you pick the ratio.

What net metering and your sanctioned load allow

  • The inverter must fit your sanctioned load. Under net metering, the inverter's AC capacity should not exceed your connection's sanctioned load or the maximum your state's rules permit. If you want a bigger system, apply for a load enhancement first; your bill shows the current sanctioned load.
  • Up to 10% extra panel capacity. Some applications were being rejected when the panels came out slightly above the approved size, for example 3.24 kW of panels on a 3 kW inverter. In an advisory issued in March 2026, MNRE recommended that DISCOMs allow DC capacity up to 10% above the applied system capacity, provided the inverter's AC capacity stays within the sanctioned load or permitted limit. Its own example: for a 5 kW system with 540 W modules, either 9 modules (4.86 kW) or 10 modules (5.40 kW) with the inverter limited to 5 kW AC. It is an advisory, so confirm with your DISCOM how it applies.
  • The subsidy is based on the panels, not the inverter. PM Surya Ghar support is worked out on installed panel capacity: ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW, ₹18,000 for the third, capped at ₹78,000. A larger inverter does not raise it (see the subsidy guide).
  • Single-phase or three-phase. Small and mid-size home systems usually use a single-phase inverter; around 10 kW installers usually fit a three-phase one. The limit for solar on a single-phase connection is set by each state. Andhra Pradesh, for example, raised it from 3 kW to 5 kW in August 2026. See the 5 kW and 10 kW guides.

Off-grid and hybrid inverters are sized to your loads

An on-grid inverter only has to pass on what the panels make. An off-grid or hybrid inverter also has to run your home from the battery, so its rating is set by the largest load you run at the same time, plus the starting surge of motors such as pumps, refrigerators and air conditioners.

  • Add up the loads that run together. List what can be on at once (fans, lights, fridge, pump, an AC) and size the inverter above that total, with headroom for starting surge. The solar for AC and appliances guide gives typical running watts.
  • Check the watt rating, not only VA. Many home inverters are labelled in VA (kVA). The continuous watt rating on the datasheet is what has to cover your loads, and it is lower than the VA figure.
  • The solar input must match the array. The charge controller or MPPT input has its own limit on panel watts and voltage. The calculator sizes off-grid arrays 35% larger than on-grid for the same use: 300 units a month in Delhi needs 2.5 kW of panels off-grid against 1.9 kW on-grid, plus a 17.6 kWh battery.
  • Hybrid systems combine both jobs: grid export and battery backup. If you only need backup for a few essential circuits, the inverter can be sized to those circuits rather than the whole house (see the hybrid solar guide and off-grid guide).

What a solar inverter costs in 2026

The calculator allocates 18% of an on-grid system's benchmark cost to the inverter (about ₹11,700 per kW), and 22% for off-grid. That works out to ₹35,100 for 3 kW, ₹58,500 for 5 kW and ₹1,17,000 for 10 kW on-grid.

Market prices spread widely by brand and features. One October 2026 price survey put on-grid string inverters at roughly ₹18,000–60,000 for 3 kW, ₹28,000–85,000 for 5 kW and ₹45,000–1,20,000 for 10 kW, from budget to premium brands. It put a 5 kW hybrid inverter at about ₹65,000–1,10,000. The calculator's figures sit inside these ranges. In a full-system quote, the inverter is usually bundled; ask for its make, model and price to be itemised (see solar panel cost in India).

What to check on the inverter in a quote

  • AC rating and panel kW together, so you can see the DC:AC ratio. Check that the AC rating is within your sanctioned load.
  • Number of MPPT inputs. Two inputs let panels facing different directions, or one string that gets afternoon shade, work independently.
  • Grid-safety certification. A grid-tied inverter must shut down during a grid outage (anti-islanding) and should be certified for use in grid-connected systems in India. Ask for the certificate for the exact model.
  • Monitoring. In August 2026 MNRE directed inverter makers supplying PM Surya Ghar to store inverter data on servers in India and share daily generation data with the national portal. Ask how you will see your own generation.
  • Warranty and service. Panels carry 25–30-year output warranties; inverters usually far less, so the warranty length and a local service network matter.
  • Rules may change. As of October 2026, the government was considering requiring domestically made inverters and batteries in the next phase of PM Surya Ghar. The current phase runs to 31 March 2027. Modules already have to be on the approved (ALMM) list, but inverters are not yet covered by an equivalent list.

Frequently asked questions

What size inverter do I need for a 3 kW solar system?
For an on-grid home, a 3 kW inverter. Panel capacity a little above 3 kW, such as 3.24 kW from six 540 W modules, still works with a 3 kW inverter, and a March 2026 MNRE advisory asks DISCOMs to accept up to 10% more panel capacity than the system you applied for, as long as the inverter stays within your sanctioned load. The inverter's share of the calculator's 3 kW estimate is ₹35,100.
Can the solar inverter be smaller than the panels?
Yes, slightly. Panels seldom reach their full rating in Indian heat and dust, so 2026 industry guides put home DC:AC ratios at about 1.1–1.25, with clipping losses estimated at roughly 1–2% of yearly output or less. Under net metering, though, the inverter must fit your sanctioned load and DISCOMs may only accept about 10% extra panel capacity, so most home systems keep the inverter close to the panel kW.
Does a bigger inverter increase the PM Surya Ghar subsidy?
No. The subsidy is worked out on the installed panel capacity: ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third, capped at ₹78,000 for a home. Choose the inverter to match your panels and sanctioned load, not the subsidy.
What size inverter does a 5 kW solar system need?
A 5 kW on-grid inverter, usually single-phase where your state allows 5 kW on a single-phase connection, and a sanctioned load of about 5 kW. MNRE's own example pairs either 9 or 10 modules of 540 W (4.86 or 5.40 kW) with a 5 kW inverter. One October 2026 price survey put 5 kW string inverters at roughly ₹28,000–85,000 depending on brand.
How do I size an off-grid or hybrid inverter?
By your loads, not only your panels. Add up everything you might run at the same time, allow for the starting surge of motors such as pumps, fridges and ACs, and compare that with the inverter's continuous watt rating, which is lower than its VA rating. Then check that its solar input accepts your array. For 300 units a month in Delhi, the calculator sizes an off-grid array at 2.5 kW with a 17.6 kWh battery.
