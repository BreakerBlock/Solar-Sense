On-Grid Solar in India: Price, Subsidy & Payback

The cheapest and most common way to put solar on an Indian rooftop — and the only one eligible for the PM Surya Ghar subsidy. Here's exactly how it works, what it costs, and the one situation where it lets you down.

Best for: Urban and semi-urban homes with reliable grid supply and high electricity bills. If your area has under 2 hours of daily outage, this is almost always the right answer.
Quick answer: a home using 300 units a month needs about 1.9 kW on-grid in Delhi. In the calculator that costs ₹1,35,650, or ₹78,650 after the ₹57,000 PM Surya Ghar subsidy, saves about ₹17,300 a year and pays back in 4.6 years. Across states, payback for most homes is roughly 4–8 years, and up to about 10 where tariffs are low. There is no battery, so the system switches off during a power cut.
Delhi homes pay less than this. The calculator applies only the central subsidy. Since September 2026 Delhi adds up to ₹26,000 per kW of its own (capped at ₹78,000, and never more than the system cost minus the central subsidy). On the 1.9 kW system above that is up to ₹49,400 more, which would take the ₹78,650 down to about ₹29,250. Homes that averaged up to 400 units a month can get up to 3 kW at no cost through the government's utility-led route. See state solar subsidy top-ups for the conditions and the other states that add one.

What "on-grid" means

An on-grid (grid-tied) system connects your solar panels directly to the utility grid through a bi-directional meter. There are no batteries. When your panels produce more than you consume, the excess flows back to the grid and your meter effectively runs backwards.

How the money works

Under net metering, you're billed only on your net consumption. Generate 400 units in a month, use 300, and you pay for zero while carrying a 100-unit credit forward. Some states pay cash for surplus at a buyback rate (typically ₹2–3.5/kWh); others only offset your bill against future consumption.

Because self-consumed units save you your full retail tariff (₹5–7.5/kWh in most states) while exported units earn only the lower buyback rate, the economics are always best when you use as much of your own generation as possible — run heavy loads during daylight.

What it costs

On-grid systems run roughly ₹55,000–85,000 per kWp installed, before subsidy, depending on panel type, inverter quality, the mounting structure and your city; some budget quotes come in lower. A typical 3 kWp home system costs around ₹1.6–2.4 lakh gross. There's no battery — which is 40–50% of an off-grid system's cost — so this is the most affordable configuration by a wide margin. Where on-grid wins

  • Cheapest per kWp — no battery cost to carry
  • Subsidy eligible — grid-connected systems qualify for the PM Surya Ghar subsidy (up to ₹78,000); off-grid systems do not, and a hybrid adds a battery the subsidy doesn't pay for
  • Lowest maintenance — inverter and panels only, no battery to replace (lead-acid lasts about 3–5 years, lithium about 8–15)
  • Fast payback — about 4.4–5 years in the calculator for a 300-unit home in Delhi, Rajasthan, Haryana and Maharashtra

The one catch: power cuts

During a grid outage, an on-grid inverter shuts down automatically — even in bright sunlight. This is a mandatory safety feature called anti-islanding: it stops your system exporting power to the grid while linemen are repairing it, so they aren't electrocuted. The practical result is that a pure on-grid system gives you no backup during a blackout.

If your area has frequent, long outages (4+ hours daily), consider a hybrid system with a small battery, or a full off-grid setup.

How to get an on-grid system connected

For a subsidised home system you register on the PM Surya Ghar national portal with your electricity consumer number, apply to your DISCOM for net metering, choose a vendor registered on the portal for your DISCOM, and have DCR panels installed. The DISCOM then installs the bi-directional net meter and commissions the system, after which the subsidy is paid into your bank account. Under the 2024 amendment to the consumer rules, systems up to 10 kW need no technical feasibility study, and the DISCOM's timeline for commissioning was cut from 30 to 15 days. Frequently asked questions

How much does an on-grid solar system cost in India?
Roughly ₹55,000–85,000 per kW installed before subsidy in 2026, so about ₹1.6–2.4 lakh for 3 kW. The calculator's benchmark puts 3 kW at ₹2,09,552 with Mono PERC panels and the net meter, or ₹1,31,552 after the ₹78,000 PM Surya Ghar subsidy. A 1 kW system is about ₹75,000 before and ₹45,000 after subsidy.
What size on-grid system do I need?
Divide your monthly units by 30 and by your state's peak sun hours. In Delhi (5.5 hours) that is about 1 kW per 165 units a month: 1.9 kW for 300 units, 2.8 kW for 450 and 3.7 kW for 600. Sunnier states need slightly less and Kerala or West Bengal slightly more.
Does an on-grid solar system work during a power cut?
No. The inverter switches off when the grid fails, even in full sun, so that it cannot send power into lines that are being repaired. This is called anti-islanding and is a safety requirement. If you need backup, add a battery with a hybrid inverter.
Do I need permission from my DISCOM?
Yes. On-grid systems require a net-metering application and approval from your electricity distribution company, plus installation of a bi-directional meter. A good installer files this paperwork for you. Timelines vary from a few weeks to a couple of months by state.
Will solar cover my whole bill?
It depends on how your system size compares to your consumption and how much you use during daylight. Many homes offset 70–100% of their bill. Use our rooftop solar calculator to size it against your actual load.
What happens to extra units I don't use?
They're exported to the grid. Depending on your state, you either get a cash buyback (₹2–3.5/kWh) or a unit credit adjusted against future bills. Some DISCOMs zero out unused credits at the financial year end, so oversizing far beyond your consumption can waste generation.
