How Many Units Does a 1 kW Solar System Generate in India?

How much electricity a rooftop solar system makes decides everything else: how big a system you need, how much of your bill it cancels and how fast it pays back. Here is how many units 1 kW to 10 kW of panels generate per day, month and year in each Indian state, and why your own meter may read a little differently.

Quick answer: in India, 1 kW of rooftop solar generates about 4 units (kWh) a day, or roughly 1,400–1,600 units a year. That ranges from about 3.5 units a day in Kerala and West Bengal to about 5 in Rajasthan. A 3 kW home system makes roughly 10.5–15 units a day, or 3,800–5,500 units a year.

The formula

Units per day = system size (kW) × peak sun hours × 0.78

Peak sun hours are the number of hours a day of full-strength sunlight (1,000 W per square metre) your location receives on average. A day with 10 hours of daylight might deliver only 5 peak sun hours, because the morning and evening sun is weak. Across the states covered here they run from 4.5 to 6.5.

The 0.78 performance ratio covers real-world losses: panels lose output when they are hot, dust and bird droppings block light, and some energy is lost in wiring and in the inverter that converts DC to AC. Together these take about 22% off the panel's rated output. This is exactly how the solar calculator estimates generation, so the tables below match what it shows for your state.

For 1 kW at the Indian average of 5 peak sun hours: 1 × 5 × 0.78 = 3.9 units a day, or about 1,424 units a year.

How these figures compare with published estimates

Installer guides in India commonly quote a rule of thumb of 4–5 units a day per kW, with yearly yields of around 1,400–1,500 units per kW in cities such as Delhi and Mumbai, higher in Rajasthan and Gujarat and lower in the North-East. The calculator's figures sit in the same range: 1,570 units per kW a year in Delhi and 1,424 at the national average. Its Rajasthan figure, about 1,860 units, is at the optimistic end, so treat it as the best case for a clean, well-angled, unshaded roof.

Seasons: summer peaks, monsoon and winter dips

Generation is not the same every month. The tables show yearly averages, and the calculator also draws a month-by-month bar chart for your state. Its seasonal profiles show the pattern most owners see:

  • Monsoon states. In Kerala, July output is about half of March. In Maharashtra, July and August run at about 60% of March.
  • Tamil Nadu gets its heaviest rain from the north-east monsoon, so October to December are its weakest months, not July.
  • North India. In Delhi, December is about two-thirds of May, because of shorter days and winter haze.
  • Rajasthan has the flattest profile, with even its weakest months above 70% of the best one.

Under net metering this evens out: surplus units in sunny months are credited against your bill and can offset grid use in weaker months, subject to your DISCOM's settlement rules. See net metering explained.

What lowers generation on a real roof

  • Direction and tilt. In India, panels produce the most when they face south at a tilt close to your latitude. East- or west-facing arrays still work but make less.
  • Shade. A water tank, parapet, tree or neighbouring building that shades part of an array can cut its output far more than the shaded area suggests. Ask for a shading check before you sign.
  • Dust. Dust and pollution build up fast in dry months. Rinsing the panels with plain water every few weeks, preferably early in the morning, keeps output up.
  • Heat. Panels are rated at 25°C and lose efficiency as they get hotter, which is why a hot May can produce less per sun hour than a cool March. Leaving an air gap under the panels helps.
  • Ageing. Panels slowly lose output over the years. A widely cited US national laboratory figure is a median of about 0.5% a year, and studies of Indian sites have found faster rates in hot, dusty conditions. The calculator does not model this, so its 25-year figures are slightly optimistic.
  • Inverter downtime. An on-grid inverter shuts down during a grid outage for safety, so frequent power cuts in daylight hours cost generation. A hybrid system keeps running.

What those units are worth

Units only matter for what they save. The calculator assumes you use about 65% of your solar units yourself, each one saving your full tariff, and export the rest at your DISCOM's buyback rate. For example, a 3 kW on-grid system in Delhi (₹7/kWh tariff, ₹3.50 buyback) makes about 4,709 units a year: 3,061 used at home save ₹21,427 and 1,648 exported earn ₹5,768, so about ₹27,200 a year. After the ₹78,000 PM Surya Ghar subsidy, the calculator puts the net cost at about ₹1.32 lakh and the payback at 4.8 years. See the solar panel cost guide for prices by size.

Checking your system is performing

Your inverter app or display shows how many units the system has produced, and your net meter shows imports and exports. Compare a full year's generation with the yearly figure for your state and system size above, rather than a single day or month. If a system is consistently more than about 15–20% below the expected yearly figure even after allowing for its direction and any shade, ask the installer to check for soiling, shading, a faulty panel or string, or inverter errors.

Frequently asked questions

How many units does a 1 kW solar panel generate per day in India?
About 4 units a day on average. Using the calculator's assumptions (peak sun hours × 0.78 for real-world losses), 1 kW makes 3.5 units a day in Kerala or West Bengal, 3.9 at the 5-hour Indian average, 4.3 in Delhi or Maharashtra and 5.1 in Rajasthan. That is about 1,280 to 1,860 units a year.
How many units does a 3 kW solar system generate per month?
About 320 to 460 units a month on average over the year, depending on the state: roughly 356 at 5 peak sun hours, 392 in Delhi, Haryana or Maharashtra, 319 in Kerala or West Bengal and 462 in Rajasthan. Summer months are higher and monsoon months lower.
How many units does a 5 kW solar system produce per day?
About 19.5 units a day at 5 peak sun hours, or roughly 7,100 units a year (about 590 a month). In Rajasthan it is closer to 25 units a day and in Kerala or West Bengal about 17.5.
Do solar panels work on cloudy and monsoon days?
Yes, but output drops. Panels still produce from diffuse light, but heavy cloud can cut a day's generation sharply. In the calculator's monthly profile for Kerala, July output is about half of March, and in Maharashtra July and August are about 60% of March. Judge a system on its yearly total, not on a single day or month.
