3 kW vs 5 kW Solar System: Which Size Is Right for Your Home?

3 kW and 5 kW are the two sizes most Indian homes end up choosing between. Here is how they compare on units generated, roof space, price, PM Surya Ghar subsidy, savings and payback, state by state, and how to tell which one your bill actually needs.

Quick answer: choose by your electricity use, not by the subsidy. A 3 kW system suits homes using roughly 400–600 units a month (about 495 in Delhi) and a 5 kW system suits roughly 700–1,000 units a month (about 825 in Delhi). Both get the same ₹78,000 central subsidy, so 3 kW pays back faster: in Delhi the calculator puts it at 4.8 years for 3 kW vs 5.9 years for 5 kW. A 5 kW system only makes sense if you will use most of what it produces.

Use the units on your bill, not the rupee amount: bills include fixed charges, slab rates and taxes, so dividing the amount by one tariff only gives a rough figure. Look at a full year of bills if you can. A home that uses 800 units in May but 400 in January averages about 600, which points to 4 kW rather than 5 kW.

A system sized this way produces about 79% of a year's consumption, because of the 0.78 performance ratio for real-world losses. To cover closer to 100%, add roughly a quarter to the size. See how many units 1 kW of solar generates for the state-by-state figures behind the table.

Why the subsidy favours 3 kW

The PM Surya Ghar central subsidy is ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kW, and it stops at ₹78,000 at 3 kW. A 5 kW home system gets the same ₹78,000 as a 3 kW one. So the subsidy covers about 37% of the calculator's 3 kW price but only about 23% of the 5 kW price, and the net cost per kW rises from about ₹43,900 to about ₹53,200. Details and how to claim it are in the PM Surya Ghar subsidy guide.

That does not make 5 kW a bad buy. It means the extra 2 kW is paid for in full, so it has to earn its keep from the units it produces.

Is the extra 2 kW worth it?

Going from 3 kW to 5 kW in Delhi adds ₹1,34,368 to the net cost (₹2,65,920 − ₹1,31,552). What it earns back depends on whether you use the extra units:

  • If your home really uses ~825 units a month, the calculator's yearly benefit rises from ₹27,195 to ₹45,108, an extra ₹17,913 a year. The extra 2 kW pays for itself in about 7.5 years, well within the 25-year life of the panels.
  • If your home uses ~495 units a month and you install 5 kW anyway, most of the extra ~3,100 units a year are surplus. Under net metering, surplus beyond your own use is usually settled at the DISCOM's buyback rate rather than your tariff. At Delhi's ₹3.50 that is about ₹10,900 a year, so the extra 2 kW takes roughly 12 years to pay back. Settlement rules vary by state, so check yours in net metering explained.

In short: buy for the consumption you have, or that you are sure is coming. Oversizing for exports alone is slow money for a home in most states.

What about 4 kW?

If your use sits between the two, 4 kW is often the right answer. In Delhi it suits about 660 units a month, costs about ₹2,76,736 before subsidy and ₹1,98,736 after it (₹49,684 per kW), saves about ₹36,257 a year and pays back in about 5.5 years. It needs about 221 sq ft of panels, or 320–400 sq ft of roof. If your use is well above 5 kW territory (1,300 units a month or more), see the 10 kW solar system guide.

Other things that decide the size

  • Sanctioned load. The national net-metering rules allow a rooftop system up to your sanctioned load (and up to 500 kW). A home with a 3 kW sanctioned load may need a load enhancement from the DISCOM before it can install 5 kW, which means an application, a one-time charge and usually slightly higher fixed charges on the bill. Some states, such as Gujarat for homes, have relaxed this limit, so check with your DISCOM before you sign.
  • Roof space. A 5 kW system needs roughly 400–500 sq ft of shadow-free roof. If you have less, higher-efficiency TOPCon panels fit more kW on the same area; see solar panel types compared.
  • Loans. Under PM Surya Ghar, collateral-free loans up to ₹2 lakh cover systems up to 3 kW at a repo-linked rate, about 6% at the October 2026 repo rate. Above 3 kW, loans are larger and priced nearer home-loan rates, roughly 8–10%. A 3 kW loan of about ₹1.89 lakh is about ₹2,094 a month over 10 years at 6%; a 5 kW loan of about ₹2.75 lakh is about ₹3,485 a month at 9%, against an average saving of about ₹3,759. See the solar loan and EMI guide.
  • Loads you are adding. An electric car or a second AC can justify the larger size. The calculator's default home EV charger entry (3.3 kW for 4 hours a day) adds about 13 units a day, roughly 400 a month, or about 2.4 kW of extra solar in Delhi. For ACs, see solar for AC and appliances.
  • Batteries. Adding storage does not change which panel size you need, but it adds a large unsubsidised cost. The hybrid solar guide explains when backup is worth it.

How to decide

  1. Add up the units (kWh) on your last 12 bills and divide by 12.
  2. Divide by 30 and by your state's peak sun hours to get the size. Or enter the units in the rooftop solar calculator, which does this and shows cost, subsidy and payback.
  3. Add capacity only for loads you are sure of, such as an EV you have ordered.
  4. Check your sanctioned load and roof space against that size.
  5. Ask for quotes at that size and compare them line by line using the checklist in the solar panel cost guide.

Frequently asked questions

Is a 3 kW or 5 kW solar system better for a home?
It depends on how many units you use. A 3 kW system suits roughly 400–600 units a month (about 495 in Delhi) and a 5 kW system roughly 700–1,000 (about 825 in Delhi). Both get the same ₹78,000 PM Surya Ghar subsidy, so 3 kW pays back faster: 4.8 years vs 5.9 years in Delhi in the calculator. Choose 5 kW only if you will use most of what it produces.
How much does a 5 kW solar system cost after subsidy?
Quotes for 5 kW typically run ₹2.75–4.25 lakh before subsidy in 2026. The calculator estimates ₹3,43,920 with Mono PERC panels, and after the ₹78,000 subsidy the net cost is about ₹2.66 lakh, compared with about ₹1.32 lakh for 3 kW.
How many units does a 3 kW and a 5 kW solar system generate per month?
At the Indian average of 5 peak sun hours, about 356 units a month for 3 kW and about 593 for 5 kW, averaged over the year. In Delhi it is about 392 and 651 units. Summer months produce more and monsoon months less.
Can I install a 5 kW solar system with a 3 kW sanctioned load?
Usually not without a load enhancement. National net-metering rules allow a rooftop system up to your sanctioned load, so most DISCOMs will ask you to raise the sanctioned load to 5 kW first, which involves an application and a one-time charge. Some states have relaxed this for homes, so check your DISCOM's current rules.
